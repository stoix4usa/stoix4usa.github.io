## Thesis

Peter's question — **what manner of persons ought ye to be?** — is answered by acquiring the character of Christ. And character is downstream of motive: **Christlike character grows out of a Christlike heart.**

## Structure

**The frame is eschatological, and that is easy to miss.** He opens on 2 Peter 3: scoffers in the last days asking *where is the promise of his coming?*; the Lord is not slack, and any seeming delay is His long-suffering mercy giving more time to repent before the great and dreadful day; **but the day of the Lord will come.** Since a new earth where righteousness prevails is coming, Peter asks what manner of persons we ought to be — and counsels that we prepare diligently now so that when He comes He finds us *in peace, without spot, and blameless*, leading lives of holiness.

**The pivot that organizes everything after it.** When we consider the Savior's character we go straight to attributes — virtue, integrity, humility, compassion, courage. But: *what is it in Jesus that gives rise to such character traits?* His answer: **they are the natural fruit of the thoughts, desires, and intents of His heart.** Therefore, to develop Christlike character **we must possess His motivations.** Attributes are not the target; they are the yield.

## The three motivations, and what each produces

| Motivation | Yields |
|---|---|
| **1. The pure love of Christ** (charity, Moroni 7:47) — pure love led Him, before the Creation, to offer Himself as Redeemer; the Atonement was and is the supreme act of love | compassion · patience · **a willingness to correct "when moved upon by the Holy Ghost"** (D&C 121:43) · a willingness to forgive |
| **2. Lifting and ministering to others** — His passion to foster the happiness and advancement of others; His whole object, in harmony with the Father, is the immortality and eternal life of God's children | humility · meekness · selflessness · courage |
| **3. Devotion to the will of God** — *my meat is to do the will of him that sent me, and to finish his work* | honor · integrity · virtue |

## Lived experience — one, and it is borrowed

**Elder Bednar's account, recounted at length.** A woman in Bednar's stake called to ask him to visit two young women being taken to the hospital after a horrific automobile accident. **At that very moment, on another phone, she received the news that her own daughter had died of injuries from the same accident.** In a calm, deliberate voice she said: *President Bednar, we must get in contact with the two other mothers. We must let them know as much as we can about the condition of their daughters and that they will soon be in the hospital.*

Bednar's observation, quoted by Christofferson: **there was no self-pity; there was no turning inward.** Her Christlike character was manifest in her *immediate and almost instinctive turning outward.* And the definition: **character is revealed in the power to discern the suffering of other people when we ourselves are suffering; in the ability to detect the hunger of others when we are hungry; and in the power to reach out and extend compassion for the spiritual agony of others when we are in the midst of our own spiritual distress.**

Then the scriptural pairing: **Jesus in agony and near death on the cross, remembering His mother and placing her in John's care.**

**Footnote counsel — three items of substance, none spoken.**
- **Note 7:** the perfection Christ speaks of **is not to be fully realized in mortality**; the invitation for the present is to *strive to incorporate* His character — so that we may look forward to His appearing **not with dread but with joyful anticipation.**
- **Note 14 — Enos's three-stage prayer.** Enos prayed first for his own spiritual welfare; then, feeling a desire for the welfare of his brethren the Nephites, poured out his soul for them; and finally prayed *with many long strugglings* for **the enemies of his people, the Lamanites.** A documented expansion sequence: self → community → adversary.

## Project alignment

**1. The Fortress Sequence's thesis, stated by a member of the First Presidency — and this is the address's single most important paragraph for the project.**

> *We tend to underestimate the influence of Christlike individuals in the world, but **working one by one has always been Jesus's approach to changing society and establishing His kingdom. It is the aggregation of individual choices over time that forms and changes societies for good or ill.** No one of us alone can change the world, but each of us can have an influence in the world.*

Set beside Oaks's Sunday-morning sentence — *only by individual reformation can an entire society eventually rise above such evils* — **two members of the First Presidency, in two consecutive sessions, state the Fortress Sequence's core claim.** Christofferson's version is the stronger of the two because it names the *mechanism*: aggregation of individual choices over time, running in **both** directions, for good **or ill**. That bidirectionality is what makes it a diagnostic rather than an encouragement, and it is exactly what the collapse-diagnostic work needs at its head.

**2. The Story of You's mechanism, corrected at the root.** *Christlike character grows out of a Christlike heart* — and therefore attributes cannot be reached by working on attributes. This is the strongest available warrant for the Headwater's claim that A→B is transformation and not behavior improvement. **Christofferson supplies the causal order: motive → attribute → conduct.** Bednar, in the same conference, supplies the same claim from the other end (transformation, not merely improved behavior). **Two apostles, two sessions, one causal chain.**

**3. The tempo hypothesis, confirmed doctrinally.** *In the case of King Benjamin's people, and some others, this spiritual rebirth with a changed heart was almost instantaneous. **For most of us, our mighty change of heart occurs incrementally over time. In either case, the outcome is the same and equally valid.***

This is the Nazareth Effect's tempo finding stated as doctrine, and it is the cleanest statement of it in the entire corpus. The sweep produced tempo data ranging from Wu deciding before the invitation was extended to Wakolo's eight years and 24 missionaries to Yee's three years of weekly drives. **Christofferson closes the question: same mechanism, different clock, equally valid outcome.** File directly to the framework.

**4. The Convergence Thesis / asymmetric model — third independent instance.** *Yes, it requires a consistent and sustained effort on our part, **but remember, it is not simply a matter of our striving.*** With Bednar (spiritual gifts are not results earned solely through focused effort and personal discipline) and Walker (the Lord does not ask for perfection all at once), **three speakers state that human labor is necessary but not sufficient.** The asymmetric position now has apostolic support from three directions in one conference.

**5. Markers.**
- **Marker 11 (Force / restrained strength) — headline.** *The meek are models of self-control, using or withholding their considerable powers and gifts as is best in every situation.* That is Marker 11's definition, and a better one than the project currently carries: **meekness as calibrated power, not absent power.**
- **Marker 4 (Equity)** — Zion as every man seeking his neighbor's interest (n. 19); Jesus not jealous of His powers but distributing them.
- **Marker 5 (Truth)** — virtue as *a search for and devotion to truth, to right thinking and right action.*
- **Marker 6 (Watchmen)** — willingness to correct *when moved upon by the Holy Ghost*, with the D&C 121:43 conditionality intact.
- **Marker 1 (Vigilance)** — *we can keep repenting to improve as we strive to follow the Lord's example.* **This is the repentance-cadence item the hunt list has carried as "partial" since the first session. It is now supplied.**
- **Marker 12 (Handoff)** — the eschatological frame makes preparation generational, not merely personal.

**6. Missionaries named as the paradigm case.** *What better example do we have than missionaries, the tens of thousands who devote months and years of their lives exclusively to serving others (often strangers) and offering, in the name of Christ, His matchless gifts of repentance and forgiveness?* — **Selflessness defined by exactly what Ben just finished doing.** The Climb should use this: the character quality his mission built is named from the First Presidency, and the question becomes how it survives the badge coming off.

**7. Bednar's 23-year through-line.** Christofferson quotes Bednar's 2003 BYU–Idaho symposium on character revealed in suffering; Bednar preaches the same claim independently in this conference (the natural man turns inward under adversity; the Savior turned outward). **A doctrine tracked across 23 years, from a symposium address to a First Presidency conference address, arriving twice in the same weekend.** Worth noting as a case study in how teaching propagates — and as a validation of the project's own practice of tracing a claim to its earliest statement.

**8. Oaks BYU devotional — seventh citation.** Gilbert, Andersen (×2), Larreal, Ortega, Holmes, and now Christofferson. **Seven citations across all four sessions, by six speakers including the Second Counselor in the First Presidency.**

## Takeaways

1. Attributes are fruit. Work the root.
2. Instantaneous for some, incremental for most — **the outcome is the same and equally valid.**
3. It is the aggregation of individual choices over time that forms and changes societies, for good **or ill.**
4. The mirrors must be finely polished.
