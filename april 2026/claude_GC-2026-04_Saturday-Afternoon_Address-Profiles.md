# 3 · Elder Gary E. Stevenson — "Lost Luggage, Redeemed Souls"
*Of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles · 11:16*

**Thesis.** Heavenly Father entrusts His most precious cargo to stewards. Ministering brothers and sisters, and everyone who nurtures the rising generation, are the baggage handlers.

**The device — and it is unusually well-researched.** He opens on the universal dread of the empty carousel, notes an estimated **33 million bags mishandled at airports in 2024**, then turns to **Kansai International Airport, Osaka**: after 30 years and hundreds of millions of pieces, **not one item of luggage lost.** How? He quotes the airport's chief of baggage operations, Tsuyoshi Habuta, whose stated conviction is that losing luggage should never happen *because luggage is precious to passengers* — an attitude that permeates his entire staff, joined to a commitment to thoroughness and attention to detail.

Then the principle worth taking: ***kaizen*** — the mindset of constantly searching for and implementing minor improvements, requiring **a quiet discipline of always looking for small ways to make processes better**, with the improvements almost always coming from those doing the everyday work. Rigorous, constant training; every bag counted, tracked, and cared for; meticulous multiple manual checks complementing the automated system. Fragile items like musical instruments hand-delivered. Bags placed on the belt **with handles facing outward** for easier retrieval.

**Lived experience.** One: his own carry-on taken at the gate, tagged, and whisked away with essential material inside; the anxious feeling through the whole flight, clutching the claim ticket, hoping it would not be forgotten, neglected, or lost. Happy ending — and the experience got him thinking.

**The turn.** He wonders what it is like for a loving Heavenly Father to send **His most precious belongings, His children,** away from their heavenly home knowing they must pass through mortality — with the comfort that they do not travel alone. Then the recognizable human scene: a parent at a curb or platform bidding farewell to a student, soldier, or **missionary** — years of teaching, preparation, and prayer culminating in placing a cherished child into the care of others, trusting conscientious stewards until reunion.

**The claim placed on us.** Not a label on a handle but a divine truth written on the heart: *you are mine, I know you, you are not alone, I have not forgotten you, I intend to bring you home* (citing Alma 40:11). **This is more than logistics. This is redemption.**

**Two named charges.**
1. **Ministering.** Scripturally aligned to Moroni 6:4 — names taken that they might be remembered and nourished by the good word of God, to keep them in the right way — and D&C 20:53, to watch over and be with and strengthen. Four contemplations offered: provide Christlike love, caring, and service; offer help and comfort in spiritual or temporal need; prayerfully seek the Spirit's guidance; help prepare families to make and keep covenants. **And the tactical note: the greatest success in missionary work comes as we act in normal and natural ways; the same is true in ministering.**
2. **The rising generation.** Bring up children in light and truth (D&C 93:40); in perilous times they need a defense and a refuge from the storm (D&C 115:6). **There is no greater need and no greater return on this investment** than with Primary children, young men, young women, and young adults.

**Scriptures.** Alma 40:11 · Moroni 6:4 · D&C 20:53; 93:40; 115:6; 20:69; 46:7; 112:14 · 2 Nephi 25:26 · Matthew 16:24 · Mark 8:34 · D&C 76:43 (n. 16).
**Authorities.** Tsuyoshi Habuta via NPR (May 2024) · **Uchtdorf, "Jesus Christ Is the Strength of Parents" (2023)** — quoted in full in note 10: provide rich nourishing soil with access to flowing heavenly water, remove weeds and anything blocking heavenly sunlight, create the best possible conditions for growth, patiently allow the rising generation to make inspired choices, and let God work His miracle · Uchtdorf, "Jesus Christ Is the Strength of Youth" (2022) · Nelson, "Hope of Israel" (2018) — the crucial spiritual skill of learning to hear the whisperings of the Holy Ghost, which gives *all* the direction one will ever need · Oaks, "The Great Plan" (2020) · First Presidency letter, Apr. 2, 2018 ("Christlike caring").

**Project alignment.**
- **Marker 12 (Handoff) — headline, and the best *institutional* statement of it in the corpus.** The luggage metaphor makes transmission a system problem with a measurable failure rate, and Kansai proves the rate can be zero when the handlers believe the cargo is precious.
- ***Kaizen* is directly usable for the Climb.** A quiet discipline of small improvements, generated by the people doing the everyday work — this is the Compound Effect with an operating culture attached, and it belongs in the practice architecture.
- **Marker 7 (Cohesion) and Marker 4 (Equity).** No one lost, no one anonymous, every bag counted.
- **The missionary-parent image is directly Ben's parents' experience**, named from the pulpit by an Apostle. Useful in the Homecoming Plan's family-facing section.
- **The caveat he inserts is important and easy to miss:** *unlike pieces of luggage, each of us is ultimately responsible for our decisions, beliefs, and actions.* The metaphor is bounded on purpose — agency is not delegated to the handlers.

**Takeaways.** (1) Thirty years, hundreds of millions of bags, zero lost — because someone decided they were precious. (2) Handles facing outward. Small things done deliberately by the people doing the work. (3) You are not luggage; you still have to choose.

# 4 · Elder Eduardo F. Ortega — "Christ—Author and Finisher of Our Faith"
*Of the Seventy · 8:29*

**The Joash diagnosis, stated plainly, in the first ten seconds. The most important address in this session for the RM instrument.**

**Opening thesis — and it is the whole point.** *Some may think that faith is inherited. True discipleship, however, always begins with a personal decision.* And then, unmistakably: **our family heritage is a great blessing, but it is not a substitute for our diligent and intentional search for a personal testimony.** Our family legacy and traditions are very helpful, but **in and of themselves they are not enough for us to build upon the rock of our Savior** (citing Helaman 5:12).

**The scriptural warrant.** Christ to Peter: *flesh and blood hath not revealed it unto thee, but my Father which is in heaven* (Matt 16:17). **Testimony does not travel by bloodline.**

**Lived experience — two generations, honestly told.**
His grandmother **Mercedes** was the pioneer in the family, joining the Church in August 1957 in Mendoza, Argentina, 32 years after South America was dedicated for the preaching of the gospel. His father — present in the Conference Center as he spoke — was six years old. He notes her faith and courage **even though her circumstances were not perfect or ideal.**
Then his own: he grew up in a home where the teachings were actively lived and the family participated in meetings and programs — **but that was not enough. I needed to know for myself.** It came while serving as a young missionary in Colombia, by experimenting upon the word (Alma 32:27) while striving to invite and help others come unto Christ.

**Addressed directly to recent converts.** He tells them that their circumstances may feel imperfect, as Mercedes's were — and that **they may become to their families and descendants what she was to us.** Then the leveling sentence: **the invitation to seek, to come to know and to do, and then to endure on the covenant path is the same for *all* of us, regardless of whether the gospel has been in our families for generations or we were baptized just last week.**

**The mountain image.** The path of discipleship often resembles climbing a mountain; the shortest path to the top is not always the most appropriate and often not the safest. **We must choose the firm and safe path, even if it is sometimes the longest and most demanding one.**

**Scriptures.** Helaman 5:12 · Matthew 16:17 · Alma 32:27 · **Moroni 6:4** (the title text — nourished by the good word of God, kept in the right way, continually watchful unto prayer, relying alone upon the merits of Christ, the author and finisher of faith) · **2 Nephi 31:19–20** (quoted in full) · Moroni 10:32 · Mosiah 5:2.
**Authorities.** **Oaks, "Coming Closer to Jesus Christ," BYU, Feb. 10, 2026** — *we need to be patient, even with ourselves; overcoming doubt can be a lengthy process, as with building faith.* **Fifth citation of that devotional in the conference.** · Rasband, "Right Before Our Eyes" (2025) · *Preach My Gospel* (2023).

**Project alignment — this is the session's highest-value item.**
- **The Joash case, inverted and named.** Joash had every external structure and no internal foundation; when Jehoiada died, it collapsed. Ortega states the doctrine that prevents it: **inherited faith is not faith.** He does not say heritage is worthless — he says it is *not a substitute.* That is exactly the distinction the Scaffolding Audit is built on.
- **Marker 12 (Handoff), from both ends at once.** Mercedes transmitted; Ortega received *and then had to acquire it himself.* The address is a working demonstration that a successful handoff still requires the receiver to do original work.
- **Moroni 6:4 is a checklist in one verse:** remembered, nourished by the good word of God, kept in the right way, continually watchful unto prayer, relying alone upon the merits of Christ. **Stevenson quotes the same verse in this session for ministering; Ortega quotes it for personal discipleship.** Two speakers, one session, one verse, two applications.
- **"The longest and most demanding path" answers the shortcut temptation** the returning missionary will face in every domain — school, career, dating, testimony.

**Takeaways.** (1) Faith is not inherited. (2) Growing up in it is a blessing and not a substitute. (3) The shortest way up the mountain is usually not the safest.

# 5 · Elder Wan-Liang Wu — "'I Will Give Away All My Sins to Know Thee'"
*Of the Seventy · 8:25*

**Thesis.** The old king of Alma 22 asked the question that decides everything: what am I willing to give up to know God?

**Lived experience — and it is the corpus's best first-contact conversion account.** Born into a non-Christian family in Taiwan. As a boy he accompanied his mother to Chinese temples to worship different gods, bringing special cakes as an offering and burning incense to ask for needed blessings — **the god of riches for work, the god of healing for health.** He loved going, because when the rituals were over he got to eat all those delicious cakes.
At ten the family moved to Bolivia and he met the missionaries. They taught him about a God he had never heard of before: living, with a glorified body of flesh and bones, his Heavenly Father, who loves him and whose literal child he is, who sent Jesus Christ so he could return. He was amazed by the account of Joseph Smith. **Though he didn't speak Spanish and couldn't understand all the words, he felt peace in his heart and somehow knew that what he was learning was good.** Before the missionaries even extended the invitation, he told his two older sisters, already members: *I've already made up my mind. I'm getting baptized.*

**The king of Alma 22, quoted at length.** Aaron reads the scriptures to him — the Creation, the commandments, the Fall, the plan of redemption prepared from the foundation of the world through Christ for whosoever would believe, through faith and repentance. The king asks what he must do. He offers to give up all he possesses and forsake his kingdom. Aaron's answer sets the conditions: bow down before God, repent of all thy sins, and call on His name in faith, believing. And the king prostrates himself and prays the sentence that titles the address — **if there is a God, and if thou art God, make thyself known unto me, and I will give away all my sins to know thee.** The story concludes not only with the king's conversion but with **the conversion of his entire household and several cities in his kingdom.**

**The question put to the congregation.** The king did not remain stuck in his state of astonishment; he chose to believe and to act. **What are we willing to give up or change to truly know God and be saved at the last day?**

**Scriptures.** Alma 22:12–18 · John 17:3 (this is life eternal, that they might know thee) · John 14:6 · D&C 58:28 (the power is in them) · Luke 18:22 · 2 Nephi 25:20.
**Authorities.** **Oaks, "Repentance and Change" (2003)** — the gospel challenges us to change; *repent* is its most frequent message; repenting means giving up all our practices — **personal, family, ethnic, and national** — that are contrary to the commandments of God; the purpose of the gospel is to transform common creatures into celestial citizens, and that requires change. · *Preach My Gospel* (2023).

**Project alignment — and there is a major convergence finding here.**
- **This is the second time in the conference that the Oaks 2003 passage is quoted.** Teh quoted it in the Saturday morning session; Wu quotes it here. **Two Seventies, two sessions, one paragraph — and it is the Marker 3 (Devotion) anchor text and the Story of You's A→B statement simultaneously.** Both men are converts or children of converts from non-Christian traditions. That is not coincidence; it is the passage that describes what happened to them.
- **Marker 3 (Devotion) — the syncretism case in its purest form.** A boy dividing petitions among a god of riches and a god of healing is the blend, literally. His account of what changed is not that he added a god but that he learned there was one.
- **Nazareth Effect.** The invitee decided *before the invitation was extended.* An unusual and valuable data point: the inviter's timing was not the operative variable.
- **The king's conditional prayer is a usable template for the seeker:** *if there is a God, and if thou art God, make thyself known unto me* — an honest prayer available to someone who does not yet believe. Directly usable for Production Line 2 and for the wanderer.
- **Terminal-zone note:** the household and several cities followed one man's decision. **Oaks's individual-reformation sentence from Sunday morning, illustrated.**

**Takeaways.** (1) He felt peace before he understood the words. (2) The king offered his kingdom; Aaron asked for his sins. (3) The question is not what you believe but what you will give away.

# 6 · Brother David J. Wunderli — "Jesus Christ Is Not Our Burden; He Is Our Relief"
*First Counselor in the Young Men General Presidency · 9:17*

**Thesis.** When the pack gets heavy, the one thing you must not remove is the Savior.

**The story — the best single teaching image in the conference.** Their son came home from Primary with a small picture of Jesus Christ. He cherished it: slept with it, protected it, carried it to school. On a family hike he **carefully placed the picture into his backpack — and packed nothing else.** At the lunch stop he collected a large pile of small, unique rocks and asked to take them home. His father said yes, but he'd have to carry them himself. He filled the pack, **carefully placed the picture of Jesus back on top, and zipped it up.**
His father lifted the heavy pack onto his back and watched him take **one step forward and five steps backward until he fell to the ground.** Undaunted, the boy opened the pack and removed three small rocks. One more try, one more fail.
Then the father, *thinking there might be a lesson to be taught*, lightheartedly suggested he remove the picture of Jesus — *that should lighten your load.*
**I will never forget the hurt look on his face.** It seemed to say: *Dad, you really want me to take Jesus out of my pack and leave Him behind?* And then the look changed to: *Dad, you're not as smart as I thought you were.*

**Second lived experience.** In West Africa, he and Diane sat with two young brothers recently joined to the Church, raised by a single mother in humble circumstances. Asked what the gospel had done for their family, sixteen-year-old **Joshua** answered with one word: **"Relief."**

**The named lie.** The enemy of happiness wants to separate you from Christ — tempting you to think the road would be easier without Him, that the weight of His commandments is too great, that the path back is too long, that repentance is too hard. **Satan is a liar. Jesus Christ is not the weight; He is the relief.**

**The four-part keeping practice — checklist-grade and explicitly daily/weekly.**
Keeping Jesus Christ with you requires **intentional, daily worship**:
1. Reverently, with real intent, kneel and pray to the Father in His name.
2. **Study the scriptures daily, especially the Book of Mormon**, reflecting on its witness — He will guide you through your mists of darkness.
3. Exercise faith, trust Him, and turn to Him in **sincere, daily repentance**.
4. **Live your covenants every day and renew them every week.**
*My young brothers and sisters, it is that simple* (Alma 37:6).

And the daily opening instruction: **each morning as you begin your daily walk, please make sure that Jesus Christ is with you.**

**The notes carry the guardrail material this project has been hunting.**
- **Note 14** applies the rocks to **addiction** by name, and quotes Nelson: as you daily immerse yourself in the Book of Mormon you can be immunized against the evils of the day, *even the gripping plague of pornography and other mind-numbing addictions.*
- **Note 8** quotes Nelson on daily repentance: nothing is more liberating or more crucial than a regular, daily focus on repentance; **repentance is not an event, it is a process** — addressed explicitly to those on the path, those who have slipped or stepped from it, and those who *can't even see the path from where they are.*
- **Note 16** quotes **Kearon, "Jesus Christ and Your New Beginning" (2025)**: to those struggling with the same sin or the same setback over and over — **He hasn't put a roadblock in front of you; He hasn't set a limit on your second chances.** Leave deliberate sinning, casual repeats, and prideful rebellion behind. *You don't have to be who you've been before.* Your second or third or fourth — or hundredth — chance.

**Scriptures.** Moses 6:34 (three times) · John 16:33 · Alma 7:11–12 · Moroni 7:41 · Alma 34:26; 37:6, 37 · Ether 12:27 · Mosiah 24:13–15; 26:30 · Matthew 11:28–30 · 3 Nephi 18; 27:27 · D&C 20:77 · 1 Nephi 8; 21:25 · Isaiah 41:10.
**Authorities.** Nelson (2017, 2019) · Kearon (2025) · Oaks, "Always Have His Spirit" (1996) · Oaks, first First Presidency interview (Oct. 2025) — *Jesus Christ is the way* · Richard G. Scott (2007).

**Project alignment.**
- **The addiction-recovery companion's core image.** Rocks accumulate *some by choice and some by the very nature of your journey* — a distinction that removes shame from half the load without excusing the other half. Paired with note 16's "hundredth chance," this is the most usable material in the conference for that production line.
- **Marker 10 (Fortification) — the media and addiction guardrail, named.** With Larreal's device counsel and Soares's digital-spaces material later this session, the standing gap is now closed from three directions.
- **Marker 1 (Vigilance).** One step forward and five steps backward is the honest picture of a life carrying too much. The counsel is not to stop walking; it is to check what's in the pack.
- **RM value, Wall 10 and Wall 1.** The four-part daily practice is the tightest version of the Climb's Part 4 anywhere in the corpus, and it comes from the Young Men presidency — the office closest to Ben's cohort.

**Takeaways.** (1) The boy took out three rocks, not the picture. (2) Relief was the whole answer, in one word, from a sixteen-year-old. (3) Some rocks you chose; some the trail gave you. Neither is a reason to leave Him behind.

# 7 · Elder Gérald Caussé — "Love All; Love Each"
*Of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles · 11:27*

**Thesis.** God's love operates on two scales at once — universal in reach, personal in application — and disciples are to do both.

**Opening.** A journalist asked what he knows now, after being ordained to the Quorum of the Twelve. **His response came instantly: I have learned that I can love even more.** He and Valérie, moved by the outpouring of love and prayers from Saints worldwide, found their own capacity to love grew in ways they never expected. **Christlike love grows and multiplies as it is shared.**

**The stars.** In the countryside after sunset he stepped outside to rest on a lounge chair. The night was so dark he could hardly see anything. Instinctively he looked up and noticed a tiny flickering point of light, then another — **and as his eyes adjusted, the sky filled with stars.** The reading: some think God is distant, and life feels heavy as a result; but if they pause to reflect on His presence they will find He is near, quietly present, far closer than they imagine. Then Psalm 8: *when I consider thy heavens… what is man, that thou art mindful of him?* — answered by verse 5, a little lower than the angels, crowned with glory and honour.

**Method — the word-pair census.** *While studying scriptures in English, I noticed dozens of verses in which the words* **all** *and* **each** *(or* **every***) appear together.* His worked example is Mosiah 27:30 — He remembereth every creature of His creating, and will make Himself manifest unto all. **Note 9 lists eighteen further references.** *Fifth independent instance of the word-census method in the conference, and the first run on a **pair** of words rather than one.*

**The doctrinal core.** *All* speaks to the universal reach of God's love; *each* testifies of His power to care for each individual soul. Most clearly manifest in the Atonement: **all** without exception will receive a full opportunity to access its blessings, **yet it is a remarkably intimate gift, tailored to each individual's needs and applied to one person at a time.** And the clarifying sentence: **to love all and to love each are not two different kinds of love but the same divine love expressed on two scales.**

**Two searching questions.**
- *Loving all:* **Are we selective, or exclusive, in determining who deserves our love?** With Matthew 5:46–47 — if ye salute your brethren only, what do ye more than others? To Him there are no strangers or outcasts, only brothers and sisters. And Joseph Smith: a man filled with the love of God is not content with blessing his family alone but **ranges through the whole world, anxious to bless the whole human race.** Discipleship was never meant to be a comfortable circle of familiar friends focused on their own interests. **We don't serve only those we already love; rather, we come to love people as we serve them.**
- *Loving each:* **Do we interact superficially, or genuinely seek to know and care for each person?** Though surrounded by multitudes, the Savior always gave His full attention to the one — one sheep, one leper, one Samaritan woman, one little child. **In His love, no one was ever lost in the crowd.** Therefore in His Church **there should be no anonymity.**

**The Beauty and the Beast reading.** He enjoys the tale — set in France, and he relates to Lumière's accent — but names it for the truth it illustrates: rather than judging the Beast by appearance, Belle learns to see beyond it, understanding that his bitterness, rude manners, and sudden anger are **only a facade, hiding a wounded soul yearning to love and be loved.** Through her patience, personal kindness, and loving care, a transformation begins deep within and ultimately changes even his outward appearance. **The application: as we extend Christlike love to each person we serve, our eyes are opened to their divine potential.**

**Scriptures.** Psalm 8:3–5 · Matthew 10:29–31; 5:46–47; 18:1–5 · Mosiah 27:30 · Moroni 6:4 · Luke 15:4–7; 17:12–19 · John 4:5–29 · 3 Nephi 17:21–24 · plus the eighteen-reference *all/each* list at note 9.
**Hymn.** **"His Eye Is on the Sparrow,"** *Hymns for Home and Church* — with its 1905 origin recounted: Civilla D. Martin asked a friend who had long endured serious health challenges how she avoided discouragement, and the friend answered that she could not be discouraged when her Father watches the sparrows and she knows He loves and cares for her.
**Authorities.** Neal A. Maxwell, "Encircled in the Arms of His Love" (2002) — **God is in the details; He knows and loves each and all of mankind** · Joseph Smith · Nelson, "Blessed Are the Peacemakers" (2002) · Caussé's own earlier addresses (2013, 2020, 2025).

**Project alignment.**
- **The macro/micro spectrum of the Nazareth Effect, stated as doctrine.** *All* and *each* are not two loves but one, expressed at two scales. **This is the framework's own axis, articulated by an Apostle** — and it belongs in the Nazareth Effect document as the clearest available warrant for treating individual and civilizational cases as instances of one mechanism.
- **Marker 7 (Cohesion) and Marker 4 (Equity).** *No anonymity* is Cohesion's operational test.
- **Marker 11 (Force / mercy).** Belle's transformation of the Beast pairs directly with **Oaks's nurse and the repulsive man** from Sunday morning: two apostles, two accounts, one claim — the love changes the one who extends it, and then the one who receives it.
- **The service-order correction is quietly important:** *we don't serve only those we already love; we come to love people as we serve them.* Love follows service, not the reverse. Directly usable for Ben's Wall 9.
- **Sparrow hymn origin is a small gem** for the Feast practice — a 1905 conversation about chronic illness producing a hymn still in use.

**Takeaways.** (1) His eyes adjusted, and the sky filled with stars. (2) All and each are the same love at two ranges. (3) You come to love people by serving them, not before.

# 8 · Elder Brian J. Holmes — "Jesus Christ Is the Way"
*Of the Seventy · 8:15*

**The single most quotable address in the conference for the Fortress Sequence and the Scaffolding Audit.**

**The image.** He and Maggie ran the **Jungfrau-Marathon** in the Swiss Alps — considered one of the world's most difficult, climbing over 6,400 feet through alpine villages and rugged high-mountain terrain. **The views carry you through the early miles**, but the final stretch turns steep and narrow, forcing runners to hike the rocky path in single file, the last ascent demanding careful attention to every step. *In this race you are not just crossing a finish line; you are conquering a mountain.*
Then the detail that makes the address: **one of the runners who finished was blind.** He ran **tethered to a guide.** For most of the race they ran side by side, but **as the course turned steeper and more demanding, the guide moved ahead, calling out every obstacle and directing each step.** Because he was bound to a guide, he accomplished what would otherwise have been impossible alone.

**The doctrine — and here is the sentence.**
Covenants bind us to our guide as the tether bound the runner. Once bound, **only we can sever the tie. Jesus never will.** As we remain tightly bound, He is bound to us; we can expect His power even on a steep stretch. But if we turn away, we have no such promise. And then:

> **Covenant blessings flow from ongoing faithfulness, not merely from past participation.**

**That is the Joash diagnosis as doctrine.** Joash had participation — a coronation, a covenant, a temple restoration, a high priest. What he did not have was ongoing faithfulness of his own, and when the scaffolding was removed the participation counted for nothing. **This one sentence should sit at the head of the Scaffolding Audit.** It also answers the returning missionary directly: two years of service is past participation. It does not accrue.

**Peter at the turning point.** When the road of discipleship became steep and many *walked no more* with Jesus, He turned to the Twelve: **Will ye also go away?** Holmes calls it a searching question that reaches through time to each of us. Peter's answer: *Lord, to whom shall we go? thou hast the words of eternal life.* **When we have doubts, when discipleship feels demanding, when others turn back — that is the moment to stay bound.**

**The apostasy mechanism, in four sentences.** Some say they don't need a prophet or a church to follow Jesus. But without apostles and prophets:
> **our perception of Christ gradually becomes whatever we want Him to be. Our understanding of doctrine shifts. Our commitment to standards erodes. Unity dissolves.**

**This is the Cycle of Apostasy compressed to four clauses, in causal order.** File it directly to *The Cycle of Apostasy: A Plain Overview* — it is a better summary of the mechanism than most of what the project has assembled, and it comes from the pulpit.

Supported by Luke 10:16 (*he that heareth you heareth me; and he that despiseth you despiseth me*) — **to knowingly reject His prophets is, by His own definition, to reject Him** — and D&C 1:16, the warning of a time when *every man walketh in his own way, and after the image of his own god.* Then Ephesians 2:20 and 4:14: the Church built on the foundation of apostles and prophets, Christ the chief corner stone, **not so that we would be controlled but so that we would be established**, no more children tossed to and fro and carried about with every wind of doctrine.

**Note 19 is a Fortress-grade observation.** Comparing 1 Nephi 8:26–27 and 11:34–36: **the great and spacious building stood high in the air — in other words, it had no foundation — and the fall thereof was exceedingly great.** And those who occupy it are described as they who fight against the Apostles of the Lamb. *A building with no foundation is precisely the scaffolding image; an Apostle-in-training makes the point in a footnote.*

**Scriptures.** John 14:6; 3:5; 6:66–69 · Luke 10:16 · Ephesians 2:20; 4:14 · D&C 1:16; 82:10; 124:45 · 2 Nephi 31:17–20 · Mosiah 3:13 · 1 Nephi 8:26–27; 11:34–36 · Moses 6:34.
**Authorities.** Oaks, "Divine Helps for Mortality" (2025) — **the strongest help God has given is a Savior** · Oaks, "What Has Our Savior Done for Us?" (2021) · Oaks, BYU Feb. 2026 (note 22) — **sixth citation** · Christofferson, "Why the Covenant Path" (2021) · Bednar, "Abide in Me, and I in You" (2023) — the covenant connection as the supernal source of perspective, hope, power, peace, and enduring joy.

# 9 · Elder Clement M. Matswagothata — "He Knows You by Name"
*Of the Seventy · 9:29*

**Thesis.** He knows each of us by name — and the most eternity-shaping name we can receive is His.

**Opening.** Walking up nervously to speak at a stake conference, his daughter tried to calm him: *Dad, don't worry about making a mistake. No one here will remember our long surname anyway.* **Matswagothata** is from Botswana; in his home language it means *getting out of a difficult situation* or *someone who can do hard things.* And — the honest admission — **while I haven't always felt like I can do hard things,** the surname is a reminder that the Savior will guide us through every season, especially the difficult ones.

**The by-name catalogue.** *I know thee by name* (Ex 33:17, to Moses). In Eden's garden, He called Adam. At the empty tomb, He called Mary. On the road to Damascus, He called Saul. And in the Sacred Grove, He called Joseph. **All by name.** *Sixth instance of the catalogue method in the sweep.*

**Lived experience — and it is the best single ministering anecdote in the conference.**
Early in his service as a young stake president, raising a family with his wife Busi, one challenge followed another. **They had just buried his mother. Two weeks later they stood at another graveside — with his counselor, mourning the counselor's teenage son.** Work pressures felt overwhelming. He began to wonder whether he was measuring up at home, at work, and before the Lord. His prayers were best expressed by the Primary song: *Heavenly Father, are you really there? And do you hear and answer ev'ry child's prayer?*
The answer came in an unexpected way. One Sunday, driving to a ward conference, he poured his heart out to the Lord. At the meetinghouse **a bright-eyed Primary boy stopped him and said, "We need to talk."** With a very serious look, almost scolding: **"When are you going to stop being a naughty boy?"** He knew he was in some kind of trouble. Before he could respond, the boy added: **"Every morning and every night, my parents ask us to pray for you."**
*I felt seen. I felt loved. I felt known. I felt that I was not alone.*

**And the line that generalizes it: just as that family prayed for me, there is someone praying for you.**

**The reframed question — and it is the address's best takeaway.**
> **The question should never be "Will the Savior walk with me?" He will. The real question is "Will I walk with Him?"**

**On casualness — the fourth speaker to name it.** *Choose to remember Him and follow Him — **not casually or occasionally but deliberately and always.*** And: as we choose to walk with Him, He is **no longer a casual acquaintance but a trusted heavenly friend.**

**Closing frame.** Whether your name represents the first generation in the Church or a multigenerational heritage of faith, **the most eternity-shaping name we can receive is the sacred name of Jesus Christ.** *We honor those who came before us, but above all we honor Him.* — **The same correction Ortega made, from the other side of the same session.**

**Scriptures.** Exodus 33:17 · Genesis 3:9 · John 20:16; 17:3; 8:12; 10:11 · Acts 9:4 · JS—H 1:17 · Alma 7:11–13 · D&C 121:1; 88:6 · Psalm 23:2 · Isaiah 40:31 (n. 16) · 3 Nephi 25:2 · Luke 18:22.
**Songs.** **"A Child's Prayer,"** *Children's Songbook* 12.
**Authorities.** **Christofferson, "Our Relationship with God" (2022)** — *in the midst of the refiner's fire, rather than get angry with God, get close to God.*

**Project alignment.**
- **Marker 7 (Cohesion) and Marker 12 (Handoff) at once.** A family teaching their children to pray by name for the stake president, twice daily — and one of those children delivering the answer to the man's private prayer. **The most compact demonstration in the corpus of how a household practice becomes another person's revelation.**
- **Marker 3 (Devotion).** The covenant identity framing — first generation or multigenerational, the operative name is His.
- **"Get close to God" rather than angry** is the Chastening/furnace counsel in five words. File to *Quotable*.
- **RM value, Wall 6 and Wall 2.** The reframed question — *will I walk with Him* — is a better daily self-check than most of the checklist, and it is short enough to keep.
- **Pastoral note:** two graves in two weeks, and the answer came through a seven-year-old with a scolding face. Worth keeping intact for the Shepherd's Companion.

**Takeaways.** (1) The question is not whether He will walk with you. (2) Someone is praying for you by name, and you may never learn who. (3) Get close to God, not angry with Him.

# 10 · Elder Ulisses Soares — "Jesus Christ—the True Vine"
*Of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles · 11:40*

**The address that closes the media-and-environment gap decisively.**

**Thesis.** *True* vine implies false ones. In a world of many voices, remaining connected to the True Vine is not merely desirable — **it is essential to our spiritual survival.**

**Doctrinal frame.** The *I am* declarations as witness of who He eternally is — the great Jehovah, the Son of God, the promised Messiah — with John 15:1, 5 spoken on the night preceding His suffering: *I am the true vine… he that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing.*

**Abiding, defined operationally — and note where he puts the digital.**
Abiding in Christ **is not an occasional or casual act; it is a constant, conscious, and sacred choice.** It is:
- allowing His teachings to abide in us, elevating our thoughts and **governing our words in every setting — *including the digital spaces where we so often interact***;
- purifying and consecrating our actions unto Him;
- letting daily choices be guided by covenants;
- allowing our lives to be directed by the Holy Ghost;
- **choosing to listen to His voice and the voice of His servants and following what they teach, above all the enticing voices of the world.**

**The tangled vines — the fullest media diagnosis in the conference.**
The word *true* suggests there are other vines **presenting themselves as legitimate, even appearing to represent the loving voice and word of the Savior and His gospel, while subtly and deviously leading minds and hearts elsewhere.** We live in a world filled with many voices relentlessly seeking our attention. Some speak with eloquence and influence and are well intentioned. Others are attractive in appearance but lack substance. **Still others are misleading and may even appear to be connected to Christ and His gospel.**
Over time, many of these voices **grow into tangled vines, rooted in popular philosophies and spread through various forms of media.** They promise security, happiness, or authenticity, yet they cannot satisfy the soul. **Often, they quietly introduce doubt and division — first in the mind and then in the heart — leading to spiritual loss and sorrow.** Becoming entangled may feel exciting at first, but it draws toward fleeting worldly pursuits and weakens the connection to the True and Living Vine. Then Colossians 2:8: *beware lest any man spoil you through philosophy and vain deceit, after the tradition of men, after the rudiments of the world, and not after Christ.*

**And the counsel, stated as a discipline rather than a prohibition:**
> **True wisdom in our technological day is found in using modern tools with spiritual discernment — through the Holy Ghost — without allowing them to replace the rightful voice of the True Vine.**

**Emmaus, read for the invitation.** At their destination the Savior *made as though he would have gone further* — but the disciples **constrained him, saying, Abide with us.** Soares's point: **without first the desire and then the invitation, their hearts would not have been transformed, their eyes would not have been opened, and they would not have returned to Jerusalem to bear witness.** *He would have kept walking. They had to ask.*

**Lived experience.** One, and it is a memorial. He will forever treasure the moment **Elder Jeffrey R. Holland called him by phone** to welcome him to the Quorum of the Twelve, minutes after he received the invitation to serve — feeling the Savior ministering in love through the voice of one of His anointed servants.

**Scriptures.** John 15:1, 4, 5 · Luke 24:28–29 · Colossians 2:8 · 2 Nephi 28:21 · **Mosiah 2:41** (dwell with God in a state of never-ending happiness) · 2 Corinthians 5:17 · 1 John 2:5–6 · Exodus 3:14 · Matthew 11:28–30 · D&C 84:45 · Hebrews 5:4.
**Authorities.** The Living Christ · **Oaks, "Q&A with the New First Presidency," *For the Strength of Youth*, Jan. 2026** — *Jesus Christ is the way… there shall be no other name given* (Mosiah 3:17).

**Project alignment.**
- **The media guardrail, fully supplied.** Not a prohibition but a discernment discipline, with a named failure mechanism — *doubt and division, first in the mind and then in the heart* — and an explicit warning about voices that **appear connected to Christ and His gospel.** That last clause is the one the project should carry: the dangerous vine is not the obviously hostile one.
- **Marker 3 (Devotion) at civilizational scale.** Tangled vines rooted in popular philosophies is the syncretism marker applied to an information environment.
- **Marker 6 (Watchmen).** *Above all the enticing voices of the world* — the hierarchy of voices stated explicitly.
- **Third address in the conference on *abide*** (with Gong and Wunderli), and **Mosiah 2:41 quoted here and by Wong** — the terminal-zone verse appearing in two sessions.
- **The Emmaus reading is a genuine addition to Gong's.** Gong read it for companionship; Soares reads it for **the necessity of the invitation.** Two apostles, one pericope, two sessions, complementary conclusions — a strong illustration of the word-threading method's yield.

**Takeaways.** (1) *True* vine means there are false ones, and the convincing ones look like the real one. (2) He made as though He would have gone further. They had to ask. (3) Use the tools; don't let them replace the voice.

# Session Findings

**1. The Joash problem is named three times, by three speakers, in two sessions.**
- **Ortega:** *Faith is not inherited… family heritage is not a substitute.*
- **Holmes:** *Covenant blessings flow from ongoing faithfulness, not merely from past participation.*
- **Matswagothata:** *We honor those who came before us, but above all we honor Him.*
This is the strongest thematic convergence in the entire sweep for the RM instrument, and none of the three speakers is quoting the others. **The Scaffolding Audit's central diagnosis is being preached independently from the pulpit.**

**2. The media-and-environment gap is closed — from four directions.** Larreal's device counsel (Sunday morning), Rowe's casualness footnote (Sunday morning), **Wunderli's named hazards** — addiction and pornography, in notes 8 and 14 — and **Soares's full information-environment diagnosis**. What began the sweep as the single conspicuous absence ends it as one of the better-supplied areas, and the material is more sophisticated than a prohibition: Soares frames it as *discernment in using modern tools*, and warns specifically about voices that **appear connected to Christ and His gospel.**

**3. The Oaks BYU devotional reaches six citations.** Gilbert, Andersen (×2, noting prior citation), Larreal, Ortega, Holmes. **Six citations, four sessions, one devotional given eight weeks before conference.** No other single source in the sweep comes close. Treat as the conference's controlling emphasis.

**4. *Casual* is the conference's named enemy — now five speakers.** Rowe (n. 13), Mutombo, Walker, Matswagothata, Soares. Most trace to the same Oaks 2024 line. **The April 2026 conference does not warn primarily against rebellion. It warns against drift.**

**5. The Oaks 2003 "Repentance and Change" passage is quoted twice** — Teh (Saturday morning) and Wu (Saturday afternoon), both Seventies from non-Christian or convert backgrounds. It is simultaneously the Marker 3 anchor and the Story of You's A→B statement.

**6. The word-census method reaches five independent instances**, plus a sixth catalogue variant: Bednar (*endure*), Gong (*abide*), Wong (*remember, remember*), Walker (*treasure*), Caussé (*all/each*), Matswagothata (calling by name). **Across all four sessions, by six different speakers, none citing another.** This is the sweep's best-validated study technique and should anchor the Feast practice.

**7. Moroni 6:4 is used twice in one session** — Stevenson for ministering, Ortega for personal discipleship. Five conditions in one verse: remembered, nourished by the good word of God, kept in the right way, continually watchful unto prayer, relying alone upon the merits of Christ.

**8. Three literary and one popular-culture citation across the conference.** Shakespeare twice (Gilbert, *Julius Caesar*; Andersen, Sonnet 116), Hawthorne (Renlund), and Beauty and the Beast (Caussé). The poems-and-literature field is confirmed as a real schema slot.

**9. Footnote counsel reaches thirteen confirmed instances.** The most consequential — Rowe's casualness confession, Yee's endowment covenants, Gilbert's sociology, Gong's promise to the youth, Wunderli's addiction material, Holmes's no-foundation observation — were all unspoken. **Any future sweep that reads body text only will lose the best material in the corpus.**

# The Sweep Is Complete

Thirty-four addresses, four sessions, four session files, one matrix. The instrument predicted its own gaps twice and closed both. The strongest single finding is the one the project did not go looking for: **three speakers, independently, preaching that inherited and past faithfulness do not carry forward.** That is the Scaffolding Audit's thesis, and it did not need to be inferred — it was said out loud.

The guardrail stands where it has stood since the first session. Holmes's sentence works in both directions: covenant blessings flow from *ongoing faithfulness*, which means the practices matter — and they flow, which means they are given rather than earned. Kept in that balance, the checklist is the daily feast made specific. Read as a ledger, it becomes what Joash had.
