# 1 · President Dallin H. Oaks — "Introduction"
*President of the Church · 2:40*

**Thesis.** This conference is different: it opens with a solemn assembly, held only at the first conference after a new President is called.

**Content.** He addresses over 17 million members and others interested in the Church. Explains that in a solemn assembly the First Presidency and Quorum of the Twelve are sustained by quorums and groups, and that for more than a century this has been an event of great significance. He expresses gratitude for the members' sustaining vote, prayers, and support, and prays that they be guided and prospered in their service. He closes by reaffirming testimony of the resurrected Lord and recognizing **that He is the head of this Church.**

**Talk type — a fifth category.** *Procedural / administrative.* Not a sermon. No lived experience, no method, essentially no scripture. **The schema needs this fifth type**, because the sweep will otherwise mis-file it as a thin sermon rather than a distinct artifact class.

**Project alignment.**
- **Marker 12 (Handoff).** The succession mechanism named and explained on the day it operates. The plainest institutional-handoff artifact in the corpus.
- **The anti-Joash statement, at institutional scale.** On the morning a new President is sustained, the first thing said from the pulpit is that Christ is the head of the Church. The structure is explicitly declared not to rest on the man being sustained. That is precisely the correction the Joash case exists to teach, stated at the moment of maximum temptation to say otherwise.
- **Marker 9 (Leadership).** Authority formally recognized, not assumed.

**Takeaway.** When leadership changes hands, the first sentence establishes what did *not* change.

# 2 · President D. Todd Christofferson — "Solemn Assembly"
*Second Counselor in the First Presidency · 9:39*

**Thesis.** Not a thesis — a liturgy. The full text of the sustaining, conducted by assignment.

**Structure — and it repays close reading.** Voting proceeds group by group, each standing in turn:
First Presidency → Quorum of the Twelve → General Authority Seventies, Area Seventies, and Presiding Bishopric → ordained patriarchs, high priests, and elders → **all members of the Relief Society, all women 18 and older** → those holding the Aaronic Priesthood only (priests, teachers, deacons) → **young women ages 11 to 18** → and finally the entire membership, everyone standing again together.

Two features worth flagging:
- **Dissent is given a channel and a name.** Those who oppose any proposal are asked to contact their stake president. The opposing vote is invited by the same sign at several points.
- **The sequence enfranchises the rising generation separately.** Aaronic Priesthood holders and young women aged 11 to 18 stand and vote as their own bodies before the whole Church votes together.

The Twelve sustained: Uchtdorf, Bednar, Cook, Andersen, Rasband, Stevenson, Renlund, Gong, Soares, Kearon, Caussé, Gilbert — with President Eyring as President of the Quorum of the Twelve and President Uchtdorf as Acting President.

**Talk type.** *Liturgical / procedural.*

**Project alignment.**
- **Marker 12 (Handoff) — the ceremony *is* the marker.** Set this beside the OT monarchy work. Judah's succession ran through assassination, a hidden infant, and a high priest crowning a seven-year-old. This is the same transfer performed with the covenant people present, voting, by name, with an opposing vote formally available. **The single best contrast artifact the project has for the Handoff marker.**
- **Marker 7 (Cohesion) enacted rather than preached.** Every group votes separately, then everyone votes together. Unity demonstrated by procedure.
- **Marker 4 (Equity).** Kearon, speaking minutes later, supplies the doctrinal gloss via Hinckley: every individual stands equal with every other in exercising the right to sustain or not to sustain.
- **RM value.** Wall 9. For Ben, "whom will you follow" is not abstract here — it is a public act with a date, a name, and a raised hand.

**Takeaway.** A covenant people transfers authority by asking every member, including the eleven-year-olds, to stand up and be counted.

# 3 · Elder Patrick Kearon — "About His Business"
*Of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles · 11:21*

**Thesis.** A calling is not an assignment to fill a vacancy. It is joining the Savior in His redeeming work — and it is tailor-made for the growth of the person called.

**Witness of and for Christ.** The twelve-year-old in the temple, about His Father's business (Luke 2:49), as the pattern for every calling. Peter, Andrew, James, and John dropping their nets — a call as inconvenient and incomprehensible as any ever issued, answered with faith. And the Apostles themselves needing the Lord's gentle reminding and His repeated invitation to feed His sheep (John 21) — **even the eyewitnesses flagged.**

**Lived experience — a single continuous narrative, and it is the richest RM material in the session.**
Baptized in his mid-twenties in London, among a host of new young single adult friends. At his first sacrament meeting after baptism he was called to the front and the congregation raised their hands to welcome him — unfamiliar to him, but he looked into those smiling faces and felt cheered on.
A week later the bishop, rocking back in his chair and thwacking a ruler into his palm, told him he had prayed about him and had a calling: assistant ward clerk. Kearon's internal reaction was that this was emphatically not him. A little grace intervened and he said thank you and that he would try to learn what was involved — he had not a clue.
The next Sunday he stood while the ward raised their hands to sustain him. **Then the ward clerk he was to be "assisting" came straight over at the end of the meeting and said he'd show him how it worked — and did, sitting side by side, often for hours, in the small clerk's office.** (He notes it was a small office for clerks, not an office for small clerks.)
The bishop later told him he had felt prompted to extend callings that would be stretching but, he trusted, not overwhelming — and in hindsight that inspiration made sense.
He also sustained many others. Sometimes the fit was obvious. Occasionally he thought, *interesting choice* — never more so than when he was the choice.

**Method.** **The retrospective calling audit.** He reviews his own service in his twenties and thirties, then invites the congregation to imagine President Oaks doing the same at that age — nothing further from possibility than the Church presidency. *Reproducible: list your callings in order and ask what each one was actually for.*

**Doctrine of common consent — unusually candid.** Not a mere formality but a mix of agency, unity, and faith; a voluntary personal commitment to support and uphold; sustaining given through prayers, love, patience, and faith. Then the line worth keeping: **will we always agree with those we sustain, or think they are serving as the Savior would? Perhaps not — and we might be right.** But as we pray for them and they for us, bridges of unity get built.

**On President Oaks.** A call he did not seek or aspire to; his father died when Dallin was seven; a faithfully resolute mother raised a resilient, hardworking son; forty-two years after the apostolic call, his whole soul responds again — **claiming no infallibility while trusting the Lord's guiding hand.**

**Scriptures.** Luke 2:49 · Matthew 4:18–22 · John 21:1–17 · Mosiah 2:17, 22–24 · D&C 28:13 · D&C 38:27 · D&C 121:34–37 · Ephesians 2:19–22 · Philippians 2:1–4 · Romans 12:4–5 · Proverbs 3:4–5 · Isaiah 55:8–11 · Mark 10:42–45.
**Hymns/poems.** None.
**Authorities.** Gordon B. Hinckley (1995) — every individual stands equal in exercising the right to sustain or not sustain · **Jeffrey R. Holland, "Lord, I Believe" (2013)** — imperfect people are all God has ever had to work with; when the infinite fulness is poured forth it is not the oil's fault that finite vessels cannot contain it all · Henry B. Eyring, "Rise to Your Call" (2002) — the person who recommended you prayed and felt an answer that you were the one · Dieter F. Uchtdorf, "Lift Where You Stand" (2008) · **General Handbook 30.0** — it is not appropriate to aspire to callings; members do not "advance" from one to another; serving faithfully matters more than what the calling is.

**Project alignment.**
- **Marker 9 (Leadership) — headline, and the marker's best supply in the corpus.** Servant-leadership defined by *how*, not *where*; aspiration explicitly ruled out; no ladder.
- **Marker 4 (Equity).** No advancing, no prominence hierarchy, and every hand weighs the same in the vote.
- **Marker 7 (Cohesion).** Common consent as the mechanism by which a body of very different people is bonded — he says outright that serving with people unlike yourself is how the body of Christ gets bonded together.
- **Marker 11 (Force / restraint).** Sustaining someone you may honestly think is doing it wrong, and praying for them anyway.
- **The Lord's economy always comes out ahead.** The direct answer to the returning missionary's coming objection that he has no time.

**Takeaways.** (1) "That is not me" is the normal first reaction, and grace usually arrives about one sentence later. (2) The most valuable person in the story is the clerk who sat down beside him for hours. (3) The calling is not the point; the *how* is.

# 4 · Sister Kristin M. Yee — "Ministering—'That Ye Love One Another; as I Have Loved You'"
*Second Counselor in the Relief Society General Presidency · 8:56*

**Thesis.** Ministering is not a program, a checklist, or a calling. We are not called to it and not released from it — it is part of the baptismal and temple covenants, and it is a way of being.

**Witness of and for Christ.** The Father carries out His eternal work by ministering to individual needs one by one; the Savior showed the pattern by blessing, healing, and caring for "the one." And the formulation that carries the address: **He atoned in indescribable agony without any assurance that we would love Him in return** — while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us (Romans 5:8). *This* is the kind of love He has, and *this* is the kind He commands (John 13:34).

**Lived experience — three, and the first is the finest tempo datum in the entire conference.**
1. **Her father.** He hit a very low point when two ministering brothers began visiting him. One invited him to come to the temple with him and his wife. He accepted. **Each week they picked him up and drove to the next city to worship and serve. This continued for three years.** Then her father decided to become a temple worker. She watched the change: he became aware and attentive to the needs of others, **took better care of his health**, began to care about his relationship with God and subsequently every relationship in his life. He had the Spirit with him and she felt it. They never judged him for where he was; they walked with him. **They are still her father's closest and dearest friends.**
2. **The nervous young sister.** She often feels anxious about ministering because she doesn't know how people will respond. Asked how she works through it, she smiled and said she goes — and it usually turns out much better than she thought.
3. **In note 14** — a wise bishop, when Yee was an overwhelmed Young Women president responsible for fifty-three young women: *when you bless the one, you bless the whole.*

**Footnote counsel — the most substantive in the conference.** **Note 7 lists the five endowment covenants in full**: obedience, sacrifice, gospel, chastity, and consecration, each glossed, with Talmage and *Preparing to Enter the Holy Temple* attached. **Note 29** carries Oaks's 2014 teaching on women and priesthood authority at length. Neither was spoken. *Fourth confirmation that the notes are part of the address.*

**Scriptures.** John 13:34 · Romans 5:8 · Matthew 16:25; 25:35–40; 22:37–39 · Mosiah 18:8–11 · Mosiah 5:5–12 · Moses 1:39 · 3 Nephi 27:21, 27 · 1 Nephi 17:3 · Isaiah 41:10 · Alma 18:32 · 2 Nephi 32:5 · Jacob 5:71 · 2 Nephi 2:25 · Luke 10:29–37 · D&C 112:11 · D&C 31.
**Hymns.** "Praise to the Man," Hymns 29 — sacrifice brings forth the blessings of heaven (via Oaks).
**Authorities.** Dieter F. Uchtdorf, "You Are My Hands" (2010) · Russell M. Nelson, "Ministering with the Power and Authority of God" (2018), "Sisters' Participation in the Gathering of Israel" (2018), "Overcome the World and Find Rest" (2022), "The Everlasting Covenant" (2022) · **Jeffrey R. Holland, "Be With and Strengthen Them" (2018)** — giving God a helping hand with His staggering task of answering prayers, drying tears, strengthening feeble knees · Quentin L. Cook (Christmas devotional 2024) — our love of God and fellow man is the ultimate test of the condition of our spirit · Dallin H. Oaks, "Sacrifice" (2012) and "The Keys and Authority of the Priesthood" (2014) · David A. Bednar, "Faithful Parents and Wayward Children" (2014) · Camille N. Johnson · James E. Talmage · Gary E. Stevenson.

**Project alignment.**
- **Nazareth Effect — the corpus's best tempo case.** Weekly, to the next city, for three years, before any visible turn. Set beside Wakolo's eight years and 24 missionaries and Teh's decades of weeding, the tempo range is now well-populated: *the mechanism is constant, the clock is not.* And the cost is fully specified — two people's weekly evenings for three years.
- **Marker 7 (Cohesion) and Marker 4 (Equity).** Ministering as the covenant's continuous obligation rather than an assignable task.
- **Marker 12 (Handoff).** Two men minister to a broken father; the father becomes a temple worker; the daughter watches; the daughter tells 17 million people. Four-stage transmission, fully documented.
- **Marker 11 (Force / restraint).** *They didn't judge him for where he was in his life.* The whole method in one clause.
- **Health as downstream.** Her father took better care of his health as a *consequence* of the spiritual change, not as a precondition. Fills a standing gap on the hunt list with an unexpected direction of causation.
- **"I find the Savior when I minister."** The reciprocal — this is the answer to Wall 7 as a *self*-interested practice, not only an altruistic one.

**Takeaways.** (1) Three years of weekly driving is what "ministering" cost in this case, and it worked. (2) The two brothers are still his closest friends — the relationship was not a project with an exit. (3) When you bless the one, you bless the whole.

# 5 · Elder Clark G. Gilbert — "Come Home"
*Of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles · 13:08*

**The single most important address in this conference for the wanderer's-return half of the cohort. Confirmed on reading.**

**Thesis.** Christ is Redeemer and **Repairer** (Isaiah 58:12) — He repairs the breaches. Four named barriers keep people from answering the call home, and each has a case.

**Witness of and for Christ.** Opening pledge, echoing Oaks's own first apostolic message of 1984: to devote whole heart, might, mind, and strength to being a special witness of the name of Jesus Christ. Then a specific witness to two names: **Redeemer** and **Repairer**.

**Poem.** **Shakespeare, *Julius Caesar*, IV.iii.249–52** — the tide in the affairs of men, which taken at the flood leads on to fortune; omitted, the voyage is bound in shallows and miseries. *Second Shakespeare of the conference (with Andersen's Sonnet 116). The "poems" schema field is now firmly established.*

**Hymn.** **"Softly and Tenderly Jesus Is Calling," *Hymns for Home and Church*** — quoted at length and named as, to him, the very voice of the Lord calling us home.

**Lived experience — five cases, structured as a taxonomy of barriers.**

*Personal:* After their first son was born, he and Christine struggled to have more children. They found hope in Minerva Teichert's painting of a pioneer mother entering the valley with her little family, beckoning others to follow. Like her, they were pleading for their future children to gather. The children eventually came; the years of hoping and praying were difficult.

| Barrier | Case | What broke it |
|---|---|---|
| **"I don't belong"** | **Tammy Anglesey** — away 30 years. Knew something was missing but felt overwhelmed just to walk back in. Mustered courage for a temple open house and could only see an experience she would never have: no sealing, no endowment. Prompted anyway, she dressed for church one Sunday, parked the car, watched others walk in, was overcome with anxiety, drove home, changed clothes, and wept alone. **She had spent Sundays sitting alone in a church parking lot.** | **An inspired bishop sent her a note inviting her back.** Gilbert met her just after her temple endowment. |
| **"I don't measure up"** | **Luis Vargas**, San Antonio — attending with his wife Andrea, a Primary president, but convinced he wasn't good enough to be baptized and couldn't measure up to the people he saw at church. | On the temple steps: *you don't have to be perfect to be in this Church; do your best, and Christ will make up the difference.* At the end of the tour he told his wife it was time to join so he could become a better father and husband. Baptized a month later; family sealed in that temple. |
| **Doubt** | **Justin and Kenna Valdez** — they *moved away from family so it would be easier to walk away from their faith.* Kenna's squinty look when Gilbert walked in. She still had faith in the Savior and a testimony of the Book of Mormon but faced triggering issues. | **The hero of the story was their eight-year-old son, who still wanted to be baptized.** A wise stake president made the opening. The promise: **as they anchored in the things they did believe, the Lord would help with the things they didn't** — not resolving every question, but learning to recognize the Holy Ghost. Six months later Kenna texted that they were ready to be sealed. Sealed in the Pocatello Idaho Temple. |
| **Trapped in traditions** | **John Raass** — former BYU football star, wary of a spiritual conversation. Asked why he had never joined, he cited the need to honor the faith traditions of his deceased parents. | They helped him see that his parents now understood and would honor his decision. Two months later, baptized. A year later, sealed. |

**Closing image — the Marriott Center.** At President Oaks's BYU devotional (his first public message after being set apart), the hall looked completely full, but hundreds of seats were unfilled and the ushers struggled to place late arrivals. **Students who already had seats began turning on their phone flashlights to signal open space** — as if holding up a light to say, *we saved a seat just for you.*

**Counsel to the ones doing the helping.** Hold on to the truth. Keep your covenants. **Helping others requires you to stay in your covenants.** And note 16 carries Sister Runia's instruction not to chase after loved ones who feel lost — stay at the tree, keep eating the fruit, and with a smile beckon.

**Scriptures.** Isaiah 58:12 · Luke 1:68 · 2 Nephi 2:6–7 · Helaman 5:12 · D&C 107:23. *(Scripture-light by design; the cases carry the weight.)*
**Authorities.** Dallin H. Oaks, "Why Do We Serve?" (1984) · **Oaks, "Coming Closer to Jesus Christ," BYU, Feb. 10, 2026** — without the Holy Ghost you will be deceived · Oaks, Ensign College devotional (2022) on reanchoring · Russell M. Nelson, "The Everlasting Covenant" (2022) — He will never tire in His efforts, and should you stray He will help you find your way back · Nelson, "Overcome the World and Find Rest" (2022) — it is *much more exhausting* to seek happiness where you can never find it · Nelson, "Joy and Spiritual Survival" (2016) · Nelson, "Choices for Eternity" (2022) · Lawrence E. Corbridge, "Stand Forever" (2019) — primary and secondary questions · Robert S. Wood (2003) · Tamara W. Runia (2023) · Jeffrey R. Holland (2021, via note 15).

**Empirical citations — a new category, and a significant one.**
The footnotes carry sociological research, cited by an Apostle to characterize the present moment:
- **Note 8** — Justin Dyer, *Deseret News*, Dec. 15, 2025, summarizing BYU research: Latter-day Saints retain faith at uniquely higher levels than other traditions, including among Millennials and Gen Z; and **despite narratives that disaffiliation is driven entirely by disbelief, the majority of those who step away continue to feel religion is important and remain open to returning.**
- **Note 11** — a Harvard Graduate School of Education study finding nearly two-thirds of college-aged youth say they lack purpose and most did not know what to do with their lives; and Ryan Burge on Pew data showing over two-thirds of Americans continue to think about the meaning and purpose of life despite rising disaffiliation.
- **Note 18** — the hastening (Nelson 2024, Cook 2025, Rasband 2025) held alongside the struggling, with the note that struggle is not antithetical to hastening but in many ways the fulfillment of last-days prophecy (2 Timothy 3; 1 Nephi 14; D&C 45).

**This is Two Worlds material with apostolic sourcing.** The claim that the departed have not stopped asking the questions is precisely the premise the project's secular-audience line rests on. **Add an "empirical citations" column to the matrix.**

**Project alignment.**
- **The Shepherd's Sweep, in one address.** Four barrier-types, four cases, four returns — a working taxonomy for securing and gathering.
- **Marker 3 (Devotion) from the opposite vantage.** John Raass was held *by* inherited tradition; Teh's parents, two speakers later in the same session, **weeded** inherited tradition. Same marker, two directions, one morning. That pairing belongs in the Devotion-marker file.
- **Marker 12 (Handoff).** The Valdez case turns on an eight-year-old. The transmission ran *upward*.
- **Marker 6 (Watchmen).** An inspired bishop's note. A wise stake president's scheduling. Both are watchmen doing precisely their job, quietly.
- **Marker 7 (Cohesion).** The phone flashlights: the seat exists, and the incumbent has to signal it.
- **Nazareth Effect.** Four more case cards with barrier, inviter, tempo (30 years / a month / six months / two months), and cost.
- **The nonnegotiable for helpers:** *helping others requires you to stay in your covenants.* This is the answer to the RM's temptation to meet a wandering friend halfway.

**Takeaways.** (1) The barrier is usually not disbelief; it is belonging, adequacy, or inheritance. (2) Anchor on what you do believe and let the Lord handle the rest — the questions do not all have to be resolved first. (3) The room looks full. It isn't. Turn on your light.

# 6 · President Henry B. Eyring — "Prayers for Peace"
*First Counselor in the First Presidency · 13:13*

**Thesis.** Peace comes through continual prayer — and spiritual strength comes from prayer *sustained before the crisis*, not summoned during it.

**Witness of and for Christ.** The Last Supper promise, given to Apostles He knew He was about to leave without Him to lead, protect, and succor them: peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you, not as the world giveth (John 14:27). Quoted twice — at the opening and again at the close, framing the whole address.

**The watchman's report on the present.** The world seems in commotion: wars and rumors of wars, the economies of whole continents faltering, prophesied wickedness accelerating as the Savior's return draws nearer. And the counter-observation, which is a datum: **faithful Saints in hardship across the world have flooded heaven with prayers; in congregations and homes, prayers have become not only more numerous but more heartfelt.** *A First Presidency assessment of the current spiritual state of the covenant people — file directly to the Fortress Sequence's present-tense diagnostic.*

**The doctrine that matters most for the checklist.** The sons of Mosiah had the spirit of prophecy and the spirit of revelation because they had given themselves to much prayer and fasting (Alma 17:3). Eyring makes the point explicit: **their spiritual strength came from *continued* prayer rather than waiting to pray until a moment of crisis when they desperately needed divine help.** Consistent prayer in joyous times *and* in seasons of distress will be rewarded in His timing.

**On the form of prayer.** Opening the windows of heaven does not require many words or flowery language. Pour out your souls in private places; have your hearts drawn out in prayer continually (Alma 34:26–27). Enter the closet, shut the door; no vain repetitions; your Father knows what you need before you ask (Matthew 6:6–8).

**On prayer without words.** Alma's people would have been destroyed had they prayed openly. **They did not raise their voices; they poured out their hearts, and He knew the thoughts of their hearts.** The voice of the Lord came in their afflictions: lift up your heads and be of good comfort — the burdens were made light, they bore them with ease, and **they did submit cheerfully and with patience to all the will of the Lord** (Mosiah 24:12–15). And the pastoral extension: you may not feel like praying, or may not know what to say, but God hears the secret prayers of your heart.

**Lived experience — one, and it is the most personal moment in the conference.**
At the funeral of his wife of sixty-one years, he was surprised by a feeling of peace and almost joy. **The people at the funeral must have wondered why he was smiling.** The Lord had answered his prayer for peace with an assurance from the Holy Ghost that let him envision the happy reunion ahead with her.

**Scriptures.** John 14:27 (twice) · Psalm 9:9–10 · Luke 11:9–10 · Alma 34:26–27 · Matthew 6:6–8 · **Mosiah 24:12–15** · D&C 19:38 · **Alma 17:3** · Alma 7:27.
**Hymns.** "Sweet Hour of Prayer," Hymns 142 (note 9).
**Authorities. None.** **Second apostle in this session to argue from scripture alone** — Bednar does the same. *The pattern is now firm enough to state: senior apostles in this conference tend to cite scripture; Seventies tend to cite apostles.*

**Project alignment.**
- **Marker 1 (Vigilance) and Marker 10 (Fortification) — headline, and Wall 10's first real doctrinal warrant.** The whole architecture of the Homecoming Plan rests on setting guardrails *before* the temptations arrive. Eyring supplies the scriptural argument for building the habit ahead of the need, with a named case (the sons of Mosiah) and a named outcome (prophecy and revelation).
- **Marker 11 (Force / submission).** *They did submit cheerfully and with patience* — the surrender→submission hinge, with cheerfully as the tell.
- **Marker 2 (Memory).** He revisits the same verse at the end that he opened with. A structural demonstration of the recap-as-mnemonic method Wong ran in the other session.
- **Fasting** appears again, coupled to prayer as the joint source of revelatory capacity (Alma 17:3).
- **The unspoken prayer** is a genuine addition to the practice set — a prayer form available when speech is impossible, unwanted, or unsafe.

**Takeaways.** (1) The strength arrives from the prayers you offered before you needed them. (2) He knows the thoughts of your heart; the words are optional. (3) A man of ninety-two, smiling at his wife's funeral, is the whole doctrine demonstrated.

# The Guardrail, Restated Once

Eyring's contribution sharpens the caution rather than softening it. He does not say that prayer *produces* peace; he says the Lord *offers* His peace, and that continued prayer is the posture of one positioned to receive it. Kearon says the same about callings — the growth is tailor-made *for* us, not manufactured *by* us. Gilbert says it plainest: only Jesus Christ can fully restore that light and joy, and in the end everyone must make their own choice to come home.

The checklist is the posture. It is not the machinery. Kept that way, it is the daily feast made specific; read the other way, it becomes new scaffolding — the one thing this whole instrument exists to warn Ben about.
