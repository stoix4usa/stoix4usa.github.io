# 1 · Elder Chi Hong (Sam) Wong — "Remember, Remember"
*Of the Seventy · 8:00*

**Thesis.** God placed His reminders in the Book of Mormon, and marked them with a doubled word. Five verses carry "remember, remember," and together they state the whole plan.

**Witness of and for Christ.** Salvation comes only through the atoning blood of Christ (Helaman 5:9); He is the foundation of the entire plan of salvation and exaltation. Every promise of redemption, every hope of resurrection, every assurance of eternal life flows through Him. Easter framing: He is resurrected, He wants us to come unto Him.

**Lived experience.** One item, and it is a method: about ten years ago he was pondering three questions — what would God want us to remember, how would He let us know, where would we find the reminders — and a clear thought came that the reminders would be in the Book of Mormon, doubled the way a parent doubles a word.

**Method — two, both first-rate.**
- **The repeated-phrase census.** Take a distinctive doubled or repeated phrase and find every instance in a book of scripture; read them as a set. His five: Mosiah 2:41 · Alma 37:13 · Helaman 5:9 · Helaman 5:12 · Helaman 14:30. *Reproducible on any phrase.*
- **The recap-as-mnemonic.** He asks the congregation whether they still remember the five, then restates them as a numbered list of five propositions. Teach, then compress, then re-teach the compression.

**Scriptures.** Mosiah 2:41 · Alma 37:13 · Helaman 5:9 · Helaman 5:12 · Helaman 14:30 · Job 38:7.
**Hymns/poems.** None.
**Authorities.** Dallin H. Oaks, "Following Christ" (Nov. 2024) — the kite-and-string analogy: the string is what holds the kite against mortal winds; cut it and the kite is carried about and crashes. · David A. Bednar, "Be Still, and Know That I Am God" (May 2024) — covenants and ordinances as anchor pins and steel rods connecting a building to bedrock, secured every time we **receive, review, remember, and renew**.

**Citation posture.** Two authorities, both current apostles; no scripture outside the Book of Mormon except one Job verse.

**Project alignment.**
- **Marker 2 (Memory) — headline.** This is the Memory marker preached whole: a preserved record carrying deliberately planted reminders, delivered through a living voice. The Josiah/Huldah mechanism in modern form.
- **Marker 10 (Fortification).** The kite string is the best guardrail image in the corpus so far — the constraint is what permits the rise. Wall 10 has been the thinnest wall; this fills it.
- **Marker 3 (Devotion)** and **Marker 5 (Truth)** carried by the commandment-keeping frame.
- **Three anchors.** Transmissible Word explicit (the reminders are *in the record*); Durable Promise explicit (Bednar's anchor pins are the covenant).
- **Terminal-zone contrast.** Mosiah 2:41 (dwell with God in never-ending happiness) set directly against Alma 37:13 (cut off from His presence). Two destinations, stated as a pair, from one pulpit. Useful for Part IV.
- **Agency.** Helaman 14:30 — whosoever perisheth, perisheth unto himself; ye are free. The Nazareth Law's own logic: heaven honors the choice.

**Takeaways.** (1) When scripture doubles a word, it is flagging the sentence. (2) The string is not the opposite of the rise; it is its condition. (3) Covenants are not made once — they are received, reviewed, remembered, and renewed.

# 2 · Elder Aaron T. Hall — "I Glory in My Jesus"
*Of the Seventy · 8:29*

**Thesis.** Nephi's phrase "my Jesus" is a possessive, not a formality. Glorying in Christ deepens by knowing His names and what each one does.

**Witness of and for Christ.** The address *is* the witness — a litany of six offices, each with what He does and what the speaker rejoices in:

| Name | What He does |
|---|---|
| **Redeemer** | arms of mercy extended; "Repent, and I will receive you" (Alma 5:33); we are engraved on the palms of His hands (Isaiah 49:16) |
| **Good Shepherd** | calls, gathers, rescues, fills with love; relentlessly works to bring us home |
| **Healer** | heals death's sting, loneliness, pain, sin, the broken heart; perfect empathy; He weeps with us; invites us to be still |
| **Advocate** | pleads our cause before the Father (D&C 45:5); satisfied justice; claimed the rights of mercy |
| **Lawgiver** | commandments guide and protect; covenants bind us so He can transform, save, and exalt |
| **Deliverer** | overcame the monsters of physical and spiritual death; delivers from pains, afflictions, temptations of every kind |

**Lived experience.** Essentially none personal. **This is a third talk-type: the litany/inventory address** — neither narrative nor exposition. The schema must accommodate it; "lived experience" is empty and the value is entirely in the method and the reference set.

**Method — two, both assignable.**
- **The name inventory.** He notes 60+ names for Christ in 1–2 Nephi, works six, then hands the exercise to the hearer: *what names of Jesus Christ help you glory in Him?* Master, Counselor, Friend are offered as starters. A study assignment with a personal output.
- **Nephi's "why" catalog.** From 2 Nephi 4:20–21, 34 he pulls a four-line list of reasons — God has been my support; He led me through mine afflictions; He hath preserved me; He hath filled me with His love — closing with the forward declaration of continued trust. **A reusable template for a personal witness: four past-tense reasons, then one future-tense commitment.**

**Scriptures.** 2 Ne 33:6 · 2 Ne 4:20–21, 34 · 2 Ne 31:19, 21 · 2 Ne 26:24 · 2 Ne 1:15 · 2 Ne 9:10 · Alma 26:16 · Alma 5:33, 60 · Alma 7:11 · Alma 42:15 · Mosiah 28:4 · Moroni 7:27 · 3 Ne 10:6 · Matt 1:21 · Luke 1:47; 4:18; 19:10 · John 10:11; 11:35; 13:13 · Isaiah 9:6; 33:22; 49:16 · Ps 46:10 · Rev 17:14 · Rom 11:26 · D&C 18:11; 19:23; 29:1; 35:2; 45:3, 5; 58:42; 88:63; 93:45 · Moses 7:53 · The Living Christ.
**Hymns/poems.** None.
**Authorities.** Patrick Kearon, "God's Intent Is to Bring You Home" (2024) · Dale G. Renlund, BYU devotional (Mar. 2024) on multiple covenants — transform, save, exalt · Dale G. Renlund, "Infuriating Unfairness" (2021) · Russell M. Nelson, "The Everlasting Covenant" (2022) — an extra measure of love and mercy in covenant relationship · Dallin H. Oaks, "What Has Our Savior Done for Us?" (2021).

**Project alignment.**
- **Marker 3 (Devotion).** "My Jesus" is the undivided heart expressed as possession. The address supplies Devotion's *warm* pole, where Becerra supplied its clinical diagnosis.
- **Marker 11 (Force / mercy pole)** and **Marker 9 (Leadership)** — the Shepherd and Advocate sections.
- **Ammon as the case for the wanderer's return.** Hall specifically notes Ammon had walked a path of rebellion and sin (Mosiah 28:4) before he became the man who could not say the smallest part of what he felt. The strongest single figure in this session for the returned-and-fallen half of the RM cohort.
- **D&C 88:63 and D&C 19:23 together give a three-step practice**: draw near / seek diligently, and to do so — **learn of Him, listen to His words, walk with Him.** Checklist-grade.
- **Story of You.** Renlund's verb triple, quoted here — *transform, save, and exalt* — is the A→B sequence in three words, with the covenant as the mechanism.

**Takeaways.** (1) A name you can define is a name you can lean on. (2) Nephi's reasons are all past-tense; his trust is future-tense. That is the structure of durable faith. (3) When glorying is hard, don't reach for feeling — reach for a name.

# 3 · President Susan H. Porter — "Here Am I, Send Me"
*Primary General President · 8:44*

**Thesis.** Christ's premortal volunteering (Abraham 3:27) is a sentence any disciple can pray. Offer what you have; the multiplication is not your job.

**Witness of and for Christ.** He volunteered out of love for the Father and for us. He came, showed how to live, taught love of God and neighbor, suffered in Gethsemane the pain of all our sins and challenges, died, and was resurrected — winning the battle over everything hard and unfair. He knows you, understands you, loves you, and will walk with you.

**Lived experience — five, deliberately small.** A mother in the Caribbean whose heavy heart lifted when her young son asked her to read the Book of Mormon with him · Primary children in Ghana sharing their school lunches · sick children in a Cambodian hospital lighting up at activity kits made by Primary children · **Chloe and Eli, both 10**, speaking to a room of business leaders about how faith shapes ordinary life — they think about the gospel every day, pray for others, look for ways to help · a Primary boy at fast and testimony meeting asking everyone who loves Jesus to raise their hands, and every hand going up. Plus her own mother's Sunday dessert and her father's answer: some now *and* later.

**Method.** **Offer, then listen.** The practice is stated as two moves: pray "Here am I, send me," then listen for thoughts and ideas — and she names the likely errands (comfort someone lonely, help your family, learn what you're supposed to learn, teach someone to pray, stand for truth). The loaves-and-fishes frame (Matt 14:15–21; John 6:5–13) does the doctrinal work: the boy did not have to solve the math.

**Scriptures.** Abraham 3:27 · Matt 14:15–21; 22:36–40; 27:50; 28:5–6 · John 6:5–13 · Isaiah 53:4–5 · Alma 7:11–13; 37:6; 38:9 · Ether 4:12 · D&C 93:29; 130:18 · 1 Cor 2:9 · The Family: A Proclamation to the World.
**Hymns.** "Holding Hands Around the World," *Hymns for Home and Church* — covenant children with a gift to give.
**Authorities.** Dallin H. Oaks, "Divine Helps for Mortality" (2025) · **Ezra Taft Benson**, BYU devotional (Dec. 1974) — the long promise that men and women who turn their lives over to God find He makes more of their lives than they can: deepened joys, expanded vision, quickened minds, strengthened muscles, lifted spirits, multiplied blessings, increased opportunities, comforted souls, friends raised up, peace poured out.

**Project alignment — the standout correlation of the session.**
- **The Three Questions of Life, in order, from the Family Proclamation.** She opens with **Identity** (your spirit is much older than your body; you were born with gifts), states **Purpose** (you have a purpose and the ability to make a difference for good), and closes on **Destiny** (return to live with Him and our families). Nelson's framework, in Nelson's stated source, delivered to eight-year-olds. This is the cleanest external confirmation the Three Questions have received in this project.
- **Marker 12 (Handoff) — headline.** The entire address is a transmission event, and its evidence base is children already transmitting to adults: Chloe and Eli teaching business leaders, a boy raising a congregation's hands, a son bringing his mother to the record.
- **Marker 9 (Leadership).** "Send me" is servant-leadership's opening line. Also Marker 4 (Equity) — the errands are all toward others.
- **Marker 1 (Vigilance).** Wake each day with purpose and confidence — the daily re-posting of the watch, in Primary language.
- **Alma 37:6** — by small and simple things are great things brought to pass — is the Law of the Root in its scriptural form.

**Takeaways.** (1) The offering prayer is short and the listening is the work. (2) You are not responsible for the multiplication. (3) The most reliable evidence that a handoff succeeded is a child teaching an adult.

# 4 · Elder Neil L. Andersen — "Eternal Marriage Is an Eternal Journey"
*Of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles · 11:42*

**Thesis.** Eternal marriage is not a status achieved at an altar but a crucible of becoming. Difficulty is part of the design, not evidence of failure.

**Witness of and for Christ.** Opens at the borrowed tomb that could not hold Him — *why seek ye the living among the dead?* Then the sealing power He gave Peter (Matt 16:19), restored and exercised in temples today. Closes on Isaiah 40:28–31: He fainteth not, He giveth power to the faint, and they that wait upon the Lord mount up with wings as eagles — read in the plural, as a couple.

**Lived experience — four, and the third is the load-bearing one.**
1. **Kneeling across the altar from Kathy.** One of very few experiences from 74 years he describes as solidly cemented in his conscious mind; the words *for time and for all eternity*.
2. **The napkins.** He and Kathy had Shakespeare's Sonnet 116 engraved on their reception napkins — love is not love that alters when it alteration finds — and in those first months, as she got to know him better, he kept the leftovers around the apartment.
3. **The couple of fifty years.** Recounted through their son: crushing periods of severe depression in the mother's younger years; the son repeatedly walking in on his father on his knees, and the many Sundays his father was quietly fasting again; service given with love, patience, and humility; the promise of eternity together giving the father a perfect brightness of hope; later, in her sixties, breast cancer and neurological issues affecting her ability to walk; the two of them doubling down on covenants; *they did it together through the final day of her mortal journey.*
4. **President Packer on the phone.** Told it was their 25th anniversary and asked whether that wasn't a great accomplishment, Packer answered without pausing that it was — for Kathy.

**Poem.** **William Shakespeare, Sonnet 116.** The first poem in the corpus; the schema's "poems" field earns its place here.

**Scriptures.** Luke 24:5–6 · Matt 16:19 · D&C 132:19–20; 66:2; 131:1–4 · Isaiah 40:28–29, 31 · The Family Proclamation.
**Authorities.** Gordon B. Hinckley (1993) — the sealing power alone would have been worth all the Restoration cost · **Russell M. Nelson, "Perfection Pending" (1995)** — perfection is pending; it comes in full only after the Resurrection and only through the Lord · **Dallin H. Oaks, BYU devotional (Feb. 10, 2026)** — the adversary has become so effective at disguising truth that without the Holy Ghost you will be deceived; many obstacles lie ahead, the distractions will be many · Oaks, "The Great Plan of Happiness" (1993) and "Divorce" (2007) · James E. Faust (1993) · Brigham Young · Joseph Fielding Smith · Brian J. Willoughby, *The Millennial Marriage* (2021).

**Note on the published text.** He states in the address that he will include prophetic teachings on divorce **in the published version** — and note 14 carries them. *Schema consequence: the footnotes carry counsel that was never spoken aloud. The harvest must read notes, not just body.*

**Project alignment.**
- **Marker 7 (Cohesion) — the richest supply in the corpus.** Cohesion has been the thinnest-supplied wall; this address fills it with the fifty-year case and with the sealing doctrine underneath it.
- **Marker 12 (Handoff).** The sealing ordinance *is* the handoff instrument; and the case reaches us through the son's own account — a handoff testifying to itself.
- **Marker 8 (Integrity)** — covenants etched indelibly; **Marker 11 (Force)** — a stellar dose of patience; **Marker 6 (Watchmen)** — the Holy Ghost as the deception-detector when the disguising is that good.
- **Three anchors.** Fixed Center (the altar, the temple) and Durable Promise (the sealing) both stated directly. With Wong supplying the Word, the session states all three.
- **"Perfection Pending" is the single most useful item in this session for the returning missionary's central anxiety.** Andersen says outright that the divine attributes we seek will not all be perfected in this life. The RM who fears he is slipping is measuring against a standard scripture does not set for mortality.
- **Story of You / the crucible.** Marriage named as the *crucible of spiritual development* where sacrifice, charity, patience, and peacemaking become a greater part of us — the A→B mechanism located inside an ordinary relationship.

**Takeaways.** (1) The altar is the trailhead, not the summit. (2) Keep the napkins. (3) A father on his knees, seen by a child who didn't know he was watching, is the whole curriculum.

# 5 · Elder Quentin L. Cook — "Keys, Covenants, and Easter"
*Of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles · 11:32*

**Thesis.** Easter Sunday 1836, one week after the Kirtland Temple's dedication, the Savior appeared and Moses, Elias, and Elijah committed keys. Without Elijah's sealing keys there are no eternal families and the whole earth would be utterly wasted.

**Witness of and for Christ.** His Atonement accomplished the Father's plan and frees everyone who has ever lived from death unconditionally and from sin on condition of repentance. Personal apostolic witness at the close.

**Lived experience.** Escorting Reform, Conservative, and Orthodox rabbis and secular Jewish leaders through Utah temple open houses from 2009 with Elder Holland · explaining baptism for the dead at the font, reading 1 Kings 7:25 on the twelve oxen of Solomon's temple, and watching recognition and tears arrive · Holland overcome in the sealing room, saying he did not wish to offend but felt compelled, then testifying that Elijah had indeed come · representing the Church at the 175th anniversary of Orson Hyde's dedication of the Holy Land · learning Holland's fifteen-year work establishing the Jerusalem Center in his thirties, with both Palestinian and Jewish leaders · and two former missionary companions, now Apostles, with their wives Pat and Mary, reading the four Gospel accounts of the last days beside the Garden Tomb.

**Method — one, and it is excellent.**
**Re-inhabit the ignorance.** He asks what a 17-year-old with only a Bible would have made of Moroni's words about Elijah: *Who was this prophet? What priesthood would he reveal? Why would the whole earth be wasted?* — three real questions — and then answers each in turn. Deliberately un-knowing what you know, in order to let the text ask again. This is Nelson's Art of Asking Questions applied to one's own study, and it is the structural spine of the address.

**The counsel that matters most for the cohort:** *you do not need to physically visit Jerusalem to feel what we felt.* The confirmation comes by the Spirit through **study** — of the Savior's life, the restoration of the priesthood, and the return of the sealing keys.

**Scriptures.** D&C 2:1–3 · Malachi 4:5–6 · D&C 110:1–9, 13–16 · D&C 27:9 · D&C 128:8–9, 17 · D&C 138:2–4, 19, 47–48 · JS—H 1:27–54 · Luke 9:28–36 · 3 Nephi 25:5 · Matt 16:19 · 1 Cor 15:3–8, 29 · 1 Kings 7:23–25; 17:1 · 2 Kings 2 · Helaman 14:2 · 2 Ne 9:5–7 · Articles of Faith 1:2–3.

Eyring, "Hearts Bound Together" (2005) — Elijah's power as the greatest power God gives His children · David A. Bednar, "The Hearts of the Children Shall Turn" (2011) · Russell M. Nelson, "Rejoice in the Gift of Priesthood Keys" (2024) · Howard W. Hunter · Stephen D. Ricks, *BYU Studies* (1983) on Kirtland and Passover.

**Project alignment.**
- **Production Line 2 — the richest single feed yet.** Elijah is revered by Christians, Jews, and Muslims; the address cites the Quran (37:123–26) on Elijah calling people away from Baal to the one true God, and notes Jews have awaited Elijah for over 2,900 years and still set a place for him at the Passover seder. **This is *The Common Tongue* material — a shared prophet as an interfaith entry point, demonstrated in practice by two Apostles in a temple.**

**Takeaways.** (1) Ask the question you would have asked before you knew the answer. (2) A shared prophet is a bridge before it is an argument. (3) You don't have to stand where He stood to know that He stood there.

# 6 · Elder Taniela B. Wakolo — "Come unto Christ—Together"
*Of the Seventy · 8:37*

**Thesis.** The temple is a symbol of hope, not pressure; the gospel is a bridge to unite families, not a wedge to divide them. God keeps His promises — in His timing.

**Witness of and for Christ.** Moses's two questions (Why? By what means?) answered by the Lord's own purpose statement — to bring to pass the immortality and eternal life of man. Nephi: He denieth none that come unto Him; all are alike unto God. And the operative line: **the Savior never forces. He invites. He blesses. He fulfills promises according to faith and faithfulness.**

**Lived experience — four full cases.**
1. **New Zealand — Denny and Alex.** Childhood friends, different churches, religion never discussed while dating. After marriage: which church? Denny, the member, felt he could not keep living divided. His father's counsel was four words of practical wisdom — keep going to church, they are your family. Two years later Alex was baptized; in time they were sealed.
2. **Brisbane — a seven-year-old and his father.** The boy pleaded for his father to join so his father could baptize him. The father had attended for years with good experiences but had not stepped forward. Wakolo extended a bold invitation; it was gently declined. **The love remains, the invitation remains, the agency remains.** Sometimes the seed is planted long before it breaks the soil.
3. **Farmington, Utah — Bob and Marlene Hasenyager.** Marlene honored covenants quietly and patiently, never pressuring. A stake president asked Bob one question: was he aware that his wife longed to attend the temple but still waited, not wanting to hurt him? Bob wept, then gave his full support. Marlene was endowed and attended regularly, and Bob noticed something — not pressure, not argument, but peace. A young missionary later suggested a specific baptismal date; Bob felt warmth and joy and peace and had his answer. He was baptized, was later diagnosed with terminal cancer, and they were sealed four months before he passed away.
4. **Fiji — eight years and 24 missionaries.** A faithful wife attended alone for eight years while her husband remained uninterested and hesitant. She did not withdraw and did not resent; she served. Her **patriarchal blessing** promised that if she remained true and faithful, a day would come when her husband would take her to the temple. Then the disclosure: the wife is his own wife, Anita. *His only regret — that he was not baptized sooner.*

**Method — three.**
- **The road-sign escalation.** A stop sign; the Philippines' "Full stop"; Missouri's "Really you gotta stop." A three-beat joke making a serious claim about the value of plainness — set up by his daughter Jasmin's definition at fourteen: conference is where prophets teach doctrine so simply it cannot be misunderstood.
- **Moses's two questions.** *Why?* and *by what means?* — asked of God and answered. **Nelson's Question Hierarchy (Why → What → How) in a single scriptural instance.**
- **The withheld disclosure.** Telling the last case anonymously, then revealing it is his own marriage. The reveal converts an illustration into a witness.

**Scriptures.** Moses 1:27–33, 39 · 2 Ne 26:33 · D&C 49:15–17 · D&C 2:3 · Jacob 1:7 · Omni 1:26 · Moroni 10:32 · Exodus 3:6, 15 · Matt 22:32.
**Hymns/poems.** None.
**Authorities.** Dallin H. Oaks, "The Family-Centered Gospel of Jesus Christ" (Nov. 2025) · Gerrit W. Gong, "No One Sits Alone" (Nov. 2025).

**Project alignment — the richest Nazareth Effect yield in the corpus to date.**
- **Four complete case cards.** Each supplies inviter, invitee, mode, prior exposure, response, cost, tempo, and outcome — the per-case data points the framework asks for, delivered ready to file:
  - *Tempo data.* Two years (Alex). Eight years and 24 missionaries (Anita). Decades of quiet covenant-keeping (Marlene). Still open (Brisbane).
  - *A live rejection with the invitation intact.* The Brisbane father is a Nazareth Rejection observed without closure — rare and valuable, because most recorded cases are told from the far side.
  - *Cost paid by the inviter,* named in every case.
- **Marker 11 (Force / restrained strength) — headline.** "The Savior never forces" is the Force marker's healthy pole stated in four words. With Hall's mercy litany, the session fills the second wall the Saturday sample predicted would run short.
- **Marker 7 (Cohesion).** Minister one by one; ensure no one sits alone.

- **Third instance of the single-question method.** Becerra's Elder Cutler, Cook's imagined 17-year-old, and now the stake president's one question to Bob. Three separate speakers, three sessions apart in the program, all showing a question doing what argument could not. **This is now a corpus-level finding, not an anecdote.**

# 7 · Elder Gerrit W. Gong — "'Tis Eastertide: No One Walks Alone"
*Of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles · 11:09*

**Thesis.** The road to Emmaus is a covenant pattern. He walks with us before we recognize Him; His word makes the heart burn and His ordinance opens the eyes.

**Witness of and for Christ.** Three unrecognized Easter-day appearances laid side by side — to Mary Magdalene, who supposed Him the gardener; to the two walking; to the eleven at meat, where He showed hands and feet and ate broiled fish and honeycomb to prove He was not a spirit. Then the abiding doctrine of John 14–15: the **First Comforter** (the Holy Ghost, who teaches all things and brings all things to remembrance) and the **Second Comforter** (Christ Himself — *I will not leave you comfortless: I will come to you*). And the vine: a branch cannot bear fruit of itself; he that abideth bringeth forth much fruit, and the fruit should remain.

**Lived experience.** As a new BYU freshman on a panel with the new BYU president, who leaned over to put him at ease and explained he was wearing a red tie for his fortieth birthday and feeling old · and the story Oaks told him then, which he still remembers: a grandfather opens a chest of coins and invites the grandchildren to take all they can; they grab handfuls of the plentiful, lesser coins, while one granddaughter takes only a few — *all my coins are gold pieces.*

**Method — three.**
- **Second-person immersion.** *Imagine we are walking to Emmaus. A stranger joins us. He sees we are sad.* He narrates the pericope in first-person plural, putting the hearer inside the account before drawing any lesson. A directly reproducible scripture-study technique.
- **Etymology as doctrine.** *Emmaus* means "hot springs" — a place of healing. The destination was named before the walk began.
- **Word-threading on *abide* / *abode*** through John 14–15, the same technique Bednar ran on *endure*. **Two apostles in one conference demonstrating the same method independently.**

**Scriptures.** Luke 24:13, 19, 21, 29–36, 39, 42 · Mark 16:9, 12, 14 · John 20:11, 14–15 · John 14:6, 16, 18, 21, 23, 26 · John 15:1, 4–5, 11, 16 · 1 John 3:24 · Rev 3:20 · Moses 6:34 · D&C 20:77, 79 · D&C 88:3; 130:3 · Matt 6:33 · 2 Ne 11:5.
**Hymns — three, the most in the session.** "Abide with Me; 'Tis Eventide" (165) · "Abide with Me!" (166), quoted in note 18 · "I Know That My Redeemer Lives" (136), the closing witness.
**Authorities.** David A. Bednar, "Abide in Me, and I in You" (2023) · Dallin H. Oaks, "Good, Better, Best" (2007) · *Teachings: Joseph Smith*.

**Note 16 carries a full promise to the youth that was not spoken from the pulpit** — witness and remember Him through the sacrament, do your best to abide and walk with Him, let Easter's promises be real each day, be faithful and prayerful, kind and helpful, clean and pure, learn and grow, and His Spirit will be with you. **Second confirmation that the footnotes carry unspoken counsel.**

**Project alignment.**
- **Marker 7 (Cohesion) — the corpus's best closing line for it.** If you see someone walking alone and sad, will you walk with them. No one sits alone and no one should walk their road to Emmaus alone or unseen.
- **Marker 2 (Memory).** The sacrament covenant stated in full (D&C 20:77, 79): willing to take His name, always remember Him, keep His commandments — with the promise of His Spirit. **The weekly engine of remembering, named as an ordinance.**
- **Marker 1 (Vigilance).** *Abide* is the continuous-tense verb; the fruit *should remain*.

- **The three-times-unrecognized pattern is the pastoral gold of this address.** For anyone — RM, wanderer, or grieving saint — who does not presently feel Him: on the first Easter, three separate groups did not recognize Him while He was standing there. Absence of recognition is not absence.
- **"Good, better, best" + the gold pieces** is a discernment tool for the returning missionary's crowded new life: the plentiful coins are not the valuable ones.

**Takeaways.** (1) He was already walking with them for seven miles before they knew it. (2) The word makes the heart burn; the ordinance opens the eyes. Both, in order. (3) Fill your hands with fewer, better coins.

# One Guardrail, Restated

The framing caution from the Saturday harvest holds, and this session strengthens it. Bednar said spiritual gifts are not earned solely by focused effort and personal discipline. Andersen says the divine attributes will not all be perfected in this life. Hall's whole litany is of things done *for* us. Porter's boy did not multiply the loaves.

**A checklist read as a merit ledger recreates Joash exactly** — every wall held up perfectly from the outside, with nothing underneath. The finished artifact should carry, in Ben's language, the distinction that these practices are **the posture that keeps a man positioned to receive**, not the machinery that produces the outcome. Kept that way, the checklist is the daily feast made specific. Read the other way, it becomes new scaffolding — and scaffolding is the one thing this whole instrument exists to warn him about.
