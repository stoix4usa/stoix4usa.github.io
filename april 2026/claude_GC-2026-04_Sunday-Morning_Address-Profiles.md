# April 2026 General Conference — Address Profiles
## Sunday Morning Session (Easter morning)

*Session three of four in the General Conference Project sweep. All nine addresses profiled from the published text at churchofjesuschrist.org. Built with Nelson Harris, August 13, 2026.*

*Doctrine cited plainly. All marker-mapping, wall-mapping, and cadence assignment is **inference — Nelson's judgment senior.** Reproduction limits observed: paraphrase throughout, short quotes only, with section headings and note numbers given so verbatim text can be lifted from the source.*

**Session character.** This is the Easter session, and it behaves like one. Seven of nine addresses take the Resurrection as their explicit subject. The consequence for the sweep is that thematic diversity is low but **doctrinal density on one point is the highest in the conference** — and two addresses (Freeman, Rowe) break the pattern with material the RM instrument needs badly.

# 1 · President Dieter F. Uchtdorf — "Encounter at the Empty Tomb"
*Acting President of the Quorum of the Twelve · 12:28*

**Thesis.** The women at the tomb did not come expecting a miracle. We must each make our own way to the empty tomb, experience what it means, and then carry the witness outward.

**Witness of and for Christ.** The central move is grammatical and deliberate: because of that Sunday morning, **we can speak of Jesus Christ in the present tense.** He is not confined to the pages of a book any more than He was confined to a grave of stone; the scriptures teach not only who He *was* but who He *is*. He lives — today, at this moment — as an active, ongoing influence who guides, comforts, listens, calms fears, and wipes away tears.

**Lived experience.** Almost none, and the absence is rhetorical. He opens by saying twice that he has tried to imagine the women's grief and *can't* — a refusal to claim an experience he hasn't had. Closes with one line: he has stood at the entrance to the empty tomb and seen with spiritual eyes.

**Method — three.**
- **The present-tense test.** Read a passage about Christ and ask whether you are hearing it in past or present tense. The whole address turns on this shift.
- **The two-invitation structure.** The angels gave Mary two commands in order — *come and see*, then *go quickly and tell*. Encounter precedes witness; do not invert them.
- **The inventory question.** Not "were you there?" but **"what *have* we seen, felt, or experienced?"** Note 20 extends it with the resource list: scriptures, the Book of Mormon, latter-day prophets, the Holy Ghost, the temple.

**Why they came — the load-bearing paragraph.** They didn't come expecting a miracle, didn't come anticipating answers, didn't come with a complete understanding. **They came out of love and reverence, to perform a loving act of service, and because they knew that when the world is dark the best place to go is toward the light.** *Devotion without resolution — the single most useful sentence in this session for anyone whose questions are unresolved.*

**Scriptures.** Luke 24:1–6 · Matthew 28:5–7, 19 · Mark 16:15 · John 1:5; 14:6; 20:29 · Matthew 19:26 · Romans 8:31, 35–39 · 3 Nephi 5:3 · Mosiah 5:2 · Moses 1:4.
**Hymns.** "Upon the Cross of Calvary" (184) · "I Know That My Redeemer Lives" (136).
**Authorities.** None cited. **A third apostle arguing from scripture alone** — with Bednar and Eyring. The pattern established across earlier sessions holds firm.

**Note 13 — the most substantial footnote of the session, and a genuine find.**
He catalogues the women by Gospel (Mary Magdalene, the only person in all four; the other Mary; Salome; Joanna), observes they represented different personalities, ages, education, and classes, and then cites **Josephus (*Antiquities* 4.8.15)** on women's testimony being inadmissible for levity and boldness of their sex. He notes the scholarly argument that this *adds* credibility — a fabricated account would have led with more credible witnesses. Then the doctrinal turn: these women represent all of us; no one is above another; God looks on the heart, not on income, station, gender, education, or reputation.
**This is an apologetic argument, a classical source, and an equity doctrine, all in a footnote that was never spoken.** Sixth confirmation of the footnote-counsel finding, and the first with an external classical citation.

**Project alignment.**
- **Marker 5 (Truth) — the session's strongest supply, and Wall 5 needed it.** The empty tomb is treated as a *verifiable encounter* to be had personally, not a proposition to be assented to.
- **Marker 12 (Handoff).** Come and see, then go and tell — the transmission sequence in two imperatives.
- **Marker 4 (Equity)** via note 13, with an unusually explicit list of the distinctions God does not weigh.
- **Two Worlds.** The Josephus material and the "unlikely witnesses" argument are directly usable for the secular-audience line.
- **The unresolved-questions doctrine.** Some hesitate to commit fully because of unresolved questions about life, the gospel, or the Church; the counsel is to step forward in faith anyway, and the dawn of understanding will follow. *Pairs exactly with Gilbert's "anchor on what you do believe."*

**Takeaways.** (1) They came in the dark, without answers, because love does not wait for resolution. (2) Come and see before you go and tell. (3) The question is not what you would have seen; it is what you *have* seen.

# 2 · President Emily Belle Freeman — "Best Days and Worst Days"
*Young Women General President · 8:39*

**One of the two most useful addresses in this session for the RM instrument.**

**Thesis.** Mortality is a proving ground where best days and worst days both do their work. Testimony is not forged in a single moment; it is built day after day, across both kinds.

**Witness of and for Christ.** Christ knows both kinds of days from the inside: suffering so great an angel was sent to strengthen Him, the betrayal of a good friend, the cross. And His story also has a garden, a stone rolled away, and an empty tomb. The structural claim: **the worst day is not the last day.**

**Lived experience — one, and it is current.** Weeks before conference they began planning their daughter's wedding; then her husband Greg took a call from the oncologist confirming the cancer had returned. *Note 1 updates the reader: a PET scan in March 2026 showed signs of cancer, Greg finished six rounds of radiation just before the address, and doctors are optimistic.* Best day to worst day in a matter of minutes.

**Method — the three-part scripture frame, run on one figure.** She reads Peter's life through three lenses in sequence — **Eternal Truths, Invitations, Promised Blessings** — and shows each one containing a best day and a worst day within a few verses:
- *Eternal truths:* Peter's confession at Caesarea Philippi (Matt 16:13–17), then three verses later the rebuke and "get thee behind me" (16:21–23). **Best day to worst day in three verses.**
- *Invitations:* Peter walks on water (Matt 14:28–29); the very next verse he sees the wind and sinks (14:30). And the observation that carries the address: **Jesus was not shouting instructions from the shore or offering advice from the safety of the boat. He was in the water — within reaching distance.**
- *Promised blessings:* the denial, the rooster, and *the Lord turned and looked upon Peter* (Luke 22:61). Her reading: perhaps that look was not a look of failure but of enabling strength, meant to carry him through the trial, the cross, and the tomb — because Jesus had already told him *I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not; and when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren* (Luke 22:32). Then, after the hard days, on the shore: **feed my sheep.** Peter was to become more than a fisherman; he would become a shepherd.

**The *For the Strength of Youth* reframe — the most directly usable item.** On the worst day with Greg she opened the guide wondering whether it could actually help, and read six headings: God's plan is for you · God wants to communicate with you · Jesus Christ will help you · Walk in God's light · Temple ordinances and covenants give you greater access to God's blessings · Jesus Christ brings joy. Her conclusion: **this is not just a guide for standards; this is a guide for strength.** And the definition she pulls from it — *joy is not the absence of sorrow in your life; it is the presence of Christ in your life.*

**Scriptures.** Matthew 16:13–17, 21–23 · Matthew 14:28–30 · Luke 22:32–34, 43, 48, 61 · Luke 23:33 · John 19:41; 20:1, 5–7; 21:17 · Moses 6:34 · D&C 3:9–10 (note 10 — Joseph Smith reminded he is *still chosen, and again called*).
**Hymns/poems.** None.
**Authorities.** None. *For the Strength of Youth* (2025) quoted six times — a manual, not a person.

**Project alignment.**
- **Marker 1 (Vigilance) and the RM's central problem, solved with a figure rather than an exhortation.** Peter is the corpus's best model of a disciple whose worst days and best days were both load-bearing. *And maybe it took both for Peter to become who the Lord needed him to become.*
- **Note 11 is a quiet gem for Wall 9:** scripture says Peter was a skilled fisherman but never that he was trained as a shepherd. **The Lord called him to work he had no training for.** For a returning missionary facing a first calling he feels unqualified for, this pairs precisely with Kearon's "that is NOT me."
- **The Story of You.** Peter's history could be told as three failures — and would be true. It can also be told as walking on water, first to confess the Christ, and holder of the keys. **Both accounts are true; the second is the one the Lord wrote.** This is the A→B narrative reframe stated as clearly as anywhere in the corpus.
- **Note 10 pairs Peter with Joseph Smith** at his worst moment (D&C 3:9–10) — *thou art still chosen, and art again called to the work.* File for the wanderer's return.
- **Marker 11 (Force/mercy).** He was in the water, not on the shore.

**Takeaways.** (1) Testimony is built over days, not decided in a moment. (2) He does not coach from the boat. (3) The guide you were given as a teenager turns out to be a guide for strength, not a list of standards.

# 3 · Elder Pedro X. Larreal — "I Feel My Savior's Love"
*Of the Seventy · 7:54 · His first general conference address*

**Thesis.** The weekly sacrament, prepared for deliberately, is how the influence of the Holy Ghost gets renewed — and how the Savior's love gets *felt* rather than merely believed.

**Witness of and for Christ.** The pronoun argument, and it's the best thing in the address: at the Last Supper Christ says *my body which is given for **you*** and *my blood which is shed for **you***. Larreal points at the second person and says the Lord uses it again and again on purpose — the sacrament was given for our benefit, for you and for me. Also: He willingly volunteered to be the Advocate between us and the Father.

**Lived experience.** None. *Litany/inventory type, in the doctrinal-exposition register.*

**The device-discipline counsel — and it closes a standing gap.**
Two full sessions and eighteen addresses passed without any direct treatment of media or environment guardrails; Wall 10 has been the thinnest wall in the whole instrument. Larreal supplies the first explicit instance: during the sacrament we should strive to remove every worldly thought, and **this is not a time for reading secular books or magazines, nor a time for checking our cell phones.**
It is narrow — one ordinance, one setting — but it is direct, specific, and behavioral. **Hunt-list item partially closed. Log it.**

**Scriptures.** 3 Nephi 18:11 · 3 Nephi 9:20 (a broken heart and contrite spirit — baptized with fire and with the Holy Ghost) · Luke 22:19–20 · D&C 20:77.
**Hymns.** None in the address; Church News links "As Now We Take the Sacrament" (169) as the related hymn.
**Authorities.** Dallin H. Oaks, **"Coming Closer to Jesus Christ," BYU, Feb. 10, 2026** — quoted at length, opening the address · Oaks, "Sacrament Meeting and the Sacrament" (2008), twice · Russell M. Nelson, "Revelation for the Church, Revelation for Our Lives" (2018) — *in coming days it will not be possible to survive spiritually without the guiding, directing, comforting, and constant influence of the Holy Ghost.*

**The BYU devotional, fourth citation.** Gilbert cited it, Andersen cited it and noted it had already been quoted, and Larreal now opens his entire address with it — quoting the fuller passage, including the Nelson warning nested inside it. **Four citations across three sessions and two days.** This is no longer a pattern to note; it is the conference's controlling emphasis, and it should be treated as such in any synthesis: *the Holy Ghost is not optional equipment, because the disguising of truth is now good enough that unaided discernment fails.*

**Project alignment.**
- **Marker 2 (Memory) — the weekly engine, again.** With Gong's D&C 20:77, 79 in the Sunday afternoon session, the sacrament is now doubly established as the Memory marker's scheduled instrument.
- **Marker 10 (Fortification).** The device counsel, plus the framing of the Holy Ghost as the compass that leads to correct choices and sanctifies.
- **Marker 3 (Devotion).** Preparation *before* the ordinance rather than attention during it.
- **RM value.** Directly checklist-able and immediately actionable — the kind of specific, small, weekly behavior the Climb's Part 4 needs more of.

**Takeaways.** (1) The pronoun is *you*, and it was chosen. (2) Preparation happens before the tray arrives. (3) Put the phone away — the first explicit instance in the sweep.

# 4 · Elder Edward B. Rowe — "Choose Jesus Christ as Your Guide"
*Of the Seventy · 9:21*

**The other of the two most useful addresses in this session — and its footnote 13 is the single best RM find of the entire sweep.**

**Thesis.** Christ is a perfect *and personal* guide through a terrain seeded with hidden dangers, and He has the power not only to guide but to heal those who misstep.

**Lived experience — one, extended and precise.** As a young lawyer resolving a border dispute between countries that had been at war, he traveled on foot through the former war zone. Thousands of land mines had been laid; expert deminers were working, but **not all mine locations were known**, and people were still occasionally injured or killed. He was assigned a guide, **Winta** — from a border town that had been attacked, who escaped and then volunteered to remain in the region throughout the war to observe the opposing army. The instructions he was given: *carefully follow your guide; walk in his footsteps a few paces behind; where he goes, you go; where he steps, you step.* At first he was uncertain and apprehensive, hiking through abandoned trenches and destroyed villages. **The more they walked together and he remained safe, the more his confidence grew.**

**FOOTNOTE 13 — never spoken from the pulpit, and it is the Scaffolding Audit's diagnosis in the speaker's own words.**
He records that as the days wore on, he noticed himself **becoming *casual* in his efforts to follow his guide** — his attention would drift and he would become less careful. Then, back at camp, he would hear of someone injured or killed in the border region who had stepped on a mine, and it would resharpen his focus. And the question he has asked himself since: **why did I *ever* allow myself to become casual even for a moment when I knew that casualness could lead to casualties?**
He then applies it: there are times when we become casual in following Jesus Christ and risk stepping on spiritual land mines — and cites Oaks, *following Christ is not a casual or occasional practice; it is a continuous commitment and way of life that should guide us at all times and in all places.*

**Why this matters more than anything else in the session.** The Onward and Upward Climb's whole diagnosis is drift — not rebellion, not a decision to leave, but the slow relaxation of intensity once the external structure is removed. Rowe names the mechanism (*casualness*), names its cost (*casualties*), locates it in a setting where the danger is real but invisible, and confesses it in himself. **And it is buried in a footnote that a body-text-only sweep would have missed entirely.**

**Project alignment.**
- **Marker 1 (Vigilance) and Marker 10 (Fortification) — the pairing the instrument has been short of.** Eyring supplied Wall 10's *argument* (build before the crisis); Rowe supplies its *image and its failure mode*.
- **Marker 9 (Leadership / following).** Winta's qualification is experiential, not credentialed: he was from there, he stayed through the war, he watched. *Where he steps, you step.*
- **Marker 6 (Watchmen).** Winta is a watchman by the literal definition — he volunteered to stay and observe.
- **Confidence grows by walking, not by being told.** The more they walked together and he remained safe, the more his confidence grew — a statement about how trust is actually built, applicable to the daily feast.
- **Healing after the misstep.** He testifies that he has personally been injured by spiritual land mines and healed by Christ through faith and repentance. *For the RM who assumes a misstep ends the walk.*

**Scriptures.** Luke 18:22 · Matthew 4:19; 11:28 · Moses 6:34 · 3 Nephi 9:13–14; 18:16; 27:21 · 2 Nephi 26:33 · Mosiah 3:8, 19; 27:25–26 · Romans 3:23 · Moroni 7:27–28 · Isaiah 41:10; 43:1, 3 (note 17).
**Hymns/poems.** None.
**Authorities.** Oaks, "Following Christ" (2024) — in note 13 · Oaks, *Church News* first interview as Church President, Oct. 2025 — *Jesus Christ is the way* · Nelson, "The Lord Jesus Christ Will Come Again" (2024) and "The Everlasting Covenant" (2022), with the ***hesed*** passage quoted in full in note 11.

**Opening line worth noting.** He begins by saying that listening to President Freeman talk about good days and bad days, the thought came to him: *Ed, you're about to find out which of the two this is.* Third instance in the conference of a speaker referencing the session in progress.

**Takeaways.** (1) Casualness leads to casualties. (2) Where He steps, you step — the instruction is that literal. (3) Confidence is a product of walking, not of being persuaded.

# 5 · Elder Ronald A. Rasband — "He Is Risen"
*Of the Quorum of the Twelve · 10:21*

**Thesis.** The Resurrection is the core doctrine of the Church, and the risen Lord is known by His outstretched hands.

**Method — the hands catalogue.** The address's organizing device: he traces the Savior's hands through the ministry — the hand that rescued Peter as he sank; the hand that motioned the man at Bethesda to rise and walk; the hands that washed the disciples' feet; the hands that took bread, gave thanks, and brake it, instituting the sacrament; the hand stretched forth at Bountiful; and the promise to Isaiah of being upheld by the right hand of His righteousness. Then the Bountiful invitation to *arise and come forth* and feel the prints. Closes on **Isaiah 49:16 — graven upon the palms of my hands.** *A reproducible study technique: trace one physical image across the whole ministry.*

**Lived experience — one, and it is the emotional center.** His grandson **Paxton**, born with a very rare chromosomal deletion, lived three years. He could not speak, crawl, walk, or run after his brothers — **but his hands reached out to theirs and to the Savior with love and affection.** Rasband remembers giving him a priesthood blessing with Paxton's father, citing John 9:3, that the works of God should be made manifest in him — and says they were. Associating with Paxton, the whole family gained increased, deep, abiding trust in the Lord. *Then God reached out and took him home.* The testimony: Paxton's spirit and body will be reunited in perfect form, and his joy will be in a body resurrected **whole and perfect.**

**Scriptures.** 3 Nephi 11:7–15; 17:7, 11, 21, 25 · Luke 24:5–6, 13–32, 39 · John 3:16; 5:8, 26; 9:3; 13:5; 14:3, 19; 20:16 · Matthew 14:31 · Luke 22:19 · Alma 11:43; 22:14 · Mosiah 27:31 · Psalm 30:5 · Isaiah 41:10; 49:16 · D&C 76:17; 107:23 · Moses 7:35 · 1 Corinthians 15:6.
**Hymns.** **"He Is Risen!" (199)** — quoted in the closing.
**Authorities.** Joseph Smith (*Teachings*, ch. 3) — the fundamental principles of our religion are the testimony of the Apostles and Prophets concerning Jesus Christ · Dallin H. Oaks, "Resurrection" (2000) — the resurrection adds meaning to doctrine, motivation to behavior, and hope for the future.

**Project alignment.**
- **The terminal zone, plainly stated.** The resurrection reaches the just *and the unjust*; every knee shall bow. Useful for Part IV of the Fortress work.
- **Marker 2 (Memory).** The hands catalogue is a memory device built from a physical image.
- **Note 11 is a small, useful detail:** by tradition the women did not attend to the body immediately but waited **in honor of the Sabbath** (Luke 23:56). Sabbath observance appearing sideways, in a footnote, in an Easter narrative.
- **Note 20** distinguishes the Holy Land as a term not used in the Bible — geography and terminology precision worth having for the Cross-Faith line.
- **Pastoral value:** for anyone carrying a disability, a lost child, or a body that failed, this is the clearest promise in the conference. *Resurrected whole and perfect.*

**Takeaways.** (1) He is known by His hands, and every one of the gestures was toward someone. (2) A child who could not speak still reached. (3) Weeping may endure for a night.

# 6 · Elder Dale G. Renlund — "Because of Jesus Christ"
*Of the Quorum of the Twelve · 11:00*

**Thesis.** Not all matters are of equal value. When too many concerns compete, the answer is not more effort but a fixed focus — on Christ and His Atonement.

**The image — and it is the session's best.** Their toy poodle **Lady** would fetch a ball anytime, anyplace, for as long as anyone threw one. One day he threw **two balls at once, blue and yellow.** Lady chased the blue, saw the yellow, dropped the blue, took the yellow, thought of the blue, dropped the yellow, turned back — and repeated the cycle until she came to a screeching halt, looked back and forth between the two, **gave up, walked to her basket, and lay down.** For Lady, two balls were too many. Renlund's extension: our lives have not just blue and yellow but red and green, orange and purple, polka dots and stripes, and every combination — **and we may feel like Lady and just want to crawl back into bed.**

**The three reasons — a clean structural spine.**
1. **He conquered death.** Resurrection is universal and unconditional for everyone who comes to earth.
2. **He has both the power and the desire to save us from our sins.** And the syllogism worth keeping: *All* mankind may be saved. **If everyone, then anyone. If anyone, then even one. And if even one, then even you.** Plus: sins sincerely repented of leave **no spiritual scar, track, or trace** — no scarlet letter to wear, now or in the eternities; the entirety of the sin, not just a part, is figuratively nailed to the cross.
3. **He understands our challenges** — having taken on our griefs, sicknesses, and frailties, He empathizes perfectly. **He, and only He, can consider every factor that shapes who we are: genetics, intellectual abilities, traditions, experiences, mental and emotional conditions, and every other circumstance.**

**Scriptures.** Alma 34:31; 11:37, 40; 31:38 · Mosiah 15:7–10; 26:29–30 · Matthew 28:18 · John 14:6 · 2 Nephi 2:1–2, 8 · Ezekiel 33:12, 14–16 · Zephaniah 3:17 · D&C 58:42–43; 137:9; 138:3, 15–17 · Moroni 6:8 · Articles of Faith 1:3.
**Hymns — the most in the conference, seven.** "Where Can I Turn for Peace?" (129, twice) · "How Firm a Foundation" (85) · "Savior, Redeemer of My Soul" (112) · "It Is Well with My Soul" · "As Bread Is Broken" · "Bread of Life, Living Water" · "Behold the Wounds in Jesus' Hands" · plus **"I Will Walk with Jesus"** quoted as a four-line stanza. *Renlund argues in hymn text the way Bednar argues in scripture.*
**Literary reference.** **Nathaniel Hawthorne, *The Scarlet Letter* (1850)** — glossed in full in note 16. Third literary citation of the conference, with Gilbert's *Julius Caesar* and Andersen's Sonnet 116.
**Authorities.** Oaks, "Good, Better, Best" (2007) · Oaks, "Divine Love in the Father's Plan" (2022) · Oaks, "Strengthened by the Atonement" (2015) · Oaks, "What Has Our Savior Done for Us?" (2021) · Oaks, *Church News* interview (2025) · Nelson, "The Answer Is Always Jesus Christ" (2023) and "Joy and Spiritual Survival" (2016) · Joseph Smith · **M. Russell Ballard, "Suicide: Some Things We Know, and Some We Do Not" (1987)** — cited at note 23 for the proposition that only Christ can weigh every factor in judgment.

**Project alignment.**
- **Marker 4 (Equity) and the Wisdom Pyramid's prioritization problem.** *Not all matters are of equal value* is the "good, better, best" doctrine restated, and Lady is its best illustration in the corpus. **Pairs directly with Gong's chest of gold coins** from the Sunday afternoon session — two apostles, two images, one principle.
- **Marker 1 (Vigilance).** The failure mode Lady models is not laziness; it is **oscillation**. She was willing and energetic. She simply could not hold one object. *For a returning missionary re-entering a life with school, work, money, and dating all thrown at once, this is the precise diagnosis.*
- **The wanderer's-return doctrine, stated most cleanly here.** No scar, no track, no trace, no scarlet letter. If even one, then even you.
- **Note 23 (Ballard on suicide) and the "mental and emotional conditions" clause** are pastoral-care material for the Shepherd's Companion — an apostle stating that judgment accounts for genetics and mental health.
- **Instruction for the daily practice:** *celebrate the joyous message of Easter every day by reflecting every day on the blessings you receive because of Him.* A daily practice, stated as such.

**Takeaways.** (1) Two balls were too many, and Lady was a good dog. (2) If even one, then even you. (3) Keep your eye on the ball.

# 7 · Elder Thierry K. Mutombo — "The Joy of a Covenant Relationship with God"
*Of the Seventy · 10:19*

**The Marker 3 case of the conference, and one of the hardest accounts in the corpus.**

**Thesis.** Covenant relationship deepens divine love and holds when everything else — including inherited tradition and extended family — pushes the other way.

**Opening device.** The **paschal greeting** — *Christ is risen* / *Truly, He is risen* — named as a custom among many Christians and used to frame the whole address, with the response given as the last line before the amen. *Cross-faith material: an explicitly shared Christian practice named approvingly by a Seventy. File to Production Line 2 alongside Cook's Elijah.*

**Lived experience — and it should be handled carefully.**
He and Nathalie have been blessed with ten children. **They experienced the death of four of them at the beginning of their marriage.** After the first three, they asked the Liberty Jail question — *Heavenly Father, where art Thou? Do You hear our cries and prayers?* Then their nine-month-old, **Allan**, died; Mutombo found him in his crib, and holding his body, cried and begged for a miracle. *My prayers did not change His mind and will.*

**And then the second blow.** After the funeral, the extended families gathered and decided **without consulting them** that tradition required Nathalie and Thierry to separate, and asked him to put his wife's belongings outside the house because they had lost many children.

He went into the house and prayed for strength. The hymn came to mind — *Where can I turn for peace?* He remembered the words of their temple sealing and the promises made. He felt peace, and the Savior's hand lifting him. **He came out with empty hands** and told those assembled that Nathalie is his eternal companion, that they are striving together to build an eternal family, and that the Savior is helping them do it. They opposed the decision. *Exercising faith in Jesus Christ made me stronger.*

**Project alignment.**
- **Marker 3 (Devotion) — the corpus's severest test.** Teh's parents weeded traditions over decades. Gilbert's John Raass was held by tradition and released. **Mutombo refused a tradition, publicly, at the worst hour of his life, against his own extended family, holding nothing.** Three vantages on one marker across three sessions; this is the third and the sharpest. He quotes Oaks directly on the point: keeping covenants requires leaving *that which is highly esteemed among men* (Luke 16:15) and *the tradition of men* (Mark 7:8).
- **Marker 7 (Cohesion).** The covenant unit held against the kin group — the exact inversion of the usual pressure direction, and a case the Cohesion marker did not previously have.
- **The unanswered prayer, stated plainly.** *My prayers did not change His mind and will.* An honest datum on the Convergence Thesis: petition does not compel outcome. **Set beside Eyring's Alma-24 burdens-made-light and Freeman's Greg, this conference gives three different answers to prayer — no, later, and yes — from three speakers.** Worth a note in the synthesis.
- **The Sunday-will-come frame.** No matter how dark your days, Sunday will come *if you faithfully keep the covenants you made.* The Easter structure applied to personal grief.
- **Benson's three-part frame** (note 12): the great *test* of life is obedience to God; the great *task* is to learn the will of the Lord and then do it; the great *commandment* is to love the Lord. Clean and memorable — file to *Quotable*.
- **Satan's program named:** *play now and pay later*, and the teaching that there are never consequences or accountability. Useful for the RM's Wall 10.

**Scriptures.** 3 Nephi 9:14, 17 · D&C 121:1–2 · 2 Nephi 2:27 · Mosiah 3:19 · Luke 16:15 · Mark 7:8 · Acts 3:16 · 1 Corinthians 15:19, 22 · Ether 12:12 · Moroni 7:27–29, 34–38.
**Hymns.** **"Where Can I Turn for Peace?" (129)**, Emma Lou Thayne named by name. *Second address in this session to reach for the same hymn — Renlund cites it twice. Three total instances in one session.*
**Authorities.** Oaks, "Following Christ" (2024) — *the same passage Rowe cites in note 13* · Oaks, "Followers of Christ" (2013) · Ezra Taft Benson · Bednar, "Ask in Faith" (2008).

**Takeaways.** (1) He came out with empty hands. (2) Prayer that does not change the outcome can still change the one praying. (3) Sunday comes — but the covenant is the condition attached to that promise.

# 8 · Elder Alan R. Walker — "A Peculiar Treasure"
*Of the Seventy · 8:24*

**Thesis.** Exodus 19:5 sets two conditions for becoming the Lord's treasure — obey His voice and keep His covenant — and becoming that treasure is neither casual nor accidental.

**Lived experience.** One, and it does exact work. As a boy he was absorbed by stories of hidden treasure, secret maps, and explorers. Visiting grandparents at the base of the Andes in Mendoza, Argentina, he and his brothers spent hours in the fields searching for shiny rocks and breaking them open, hoping for something precious inside — **only ever finding more glittering minerals, never any real treasure.** The payoff comes at the end: *just as the treasures my brothers and I searched for were never found on the surface,* becoming the Lord's treasure is not found on the surface either.

**Method — the word census, again.** He notes *treasure* appears dozens of times in scripture, almost always as a warning not to set our hearts on riches — **and then isolates the one passage where the Lord calls *us* His treasure.** *Fourth instance of the word-census method in the conference* (with Bednar on *endure*, Gong on *abide*, Wong on *remember, remember*). Four speakers across four sessions, independently. **The method is now firmly validated for the Feast practice.**

**The acceptance criterion — D&C 97:8, and it is the most usable sentence in the address.** Those who **know their hearts are honest, and are broken, and their spirits contrite, and are willing to observe their covenants by sacrifice** — they are accepted of Him. Walker's gloss: **the Lord does not ask for perfection all at once.** He asks for honest and broken hearts, humble spirits, and a willing readiness — even when it costs.

**The sacrifice clause.** We must be willing to lay aside lesser things — **habits, ambitions, or comforts that distance our hearts from Him.** And: as we follow prophetic counsel *even when it stretches us or feels inconvenient*, we show we cherish Him above worldly desires.

**Scriptures.** Exodus 19:5 (twice) · Moses 1:39 · John 3:16; 15:13 · **D&C 97:8**.
**Hymns/poems.** None.
**Authorities.** Nelson, "The Everlasting Covenant" (2022) — the *never tire / never exhaust* passage, **third citation of it in the conference** (with Gilbert and Rowe) · Oaks, "Following Christ" (2024) — *enable us to soar to celestial heights*; **third citation of that address too** (with Rowe n. 13 and Mutombo) · **Christofferson, "The Power of Covenants" (2009)** — a continual stream of blessings; covenant faithfulness can *convert tribulation into triumph*; and three named consequences: the promised fruits of obedience become evident, the Spirit communicates God's pleasure, and we can face life with hope knowing we will succeed in the end because we have God's promise.

# 9 · President Dallin H. Oaks — "Alive in Christ"
*President of the Church · 13:38 · His first full address as President in general conference*

**Thesis.** Belief in a literal, universal resurrection changes the whole perspective of mortal life — and the immediate consequence is that we must love our adversaries and become peacemakers.

**Part I — the resurrection's practical consequences.** Alma 11:43–44: the restoration comes to all, both old and young, bond and free, male and female, **the wicked and the righteous**. Then the list of what the conviction *does*: it changes how we look on the physical challenges of mortality; gives strength and perspective to endure what we and those we love face; **signifies that mortal deficiencies are only temporary**; gives courage to face our own death or that of loved ones, even deaths we might call premature; and **encourages us to fulfill our family responsibilities in mortality**, living together in love in anticipation of joyful reunions.

**Part II — the diagnosis of the present.** *Many current writers characterize the time in which we live as toxic, a time of contempt or hostility toward adversaries* — and he notes this affects many relationships **involving many whose Christian beliefs should orient them otherwise.** Then the Sermon on the Mount: love your enemies, bless them that curse you, pray for them which despitefully use you. And the philological move: the full meaning of *enemies* in the sources King James's translators drew on includes military foes but **extends to any who actively oppose one another** — so today we might say we are commanded to **love our adversaries.**

**Lived experience — one, and it is the best story in the session.**
At a stake conference many years ago, looking over the audience before the meeting, he had an unusual impression to call on **a particular woman in a yellow dress.** He asked the stake president whether she would give a suitable talk; the president thought so; she was called out of the audience. As she came forward, **Oaks was apprehensive about what she might say.**
She was a nurse in a maximum-care facility. Among her patients was one she described as the most repulsive man she had ever met — from his bedridden position he did everything he could to make life miserable for his nurses: foul language, spitting on the floor, constant insults. **She despised him.**
One evening she heard a loud crash from his room, ran in, and found him fallen out of bed, thrashing in a pool of broken glass, liquid, and blood. **In that moment a profound change came over her.** She felt an almost electric current of love from Heavenly Father toward this man. She saw him as a child of God. As she knelt and held him and tried to comfort him, he said, *I want to go home. I just want to go home.* In a short time he was dead. She testified that being brought to see a despised enemy as a child of God was one of the great spiritual experiences of her life.
Oaks's own note: *for me, this was a lesson I needed to learn about our Heavenly Father's love for all His children.*

**Part III — the balance, stated with unusual candor.**
Following Christ in this does **not** mean surrendering our values; the covenants position us as devoted participants in the contest between truth and error. And then the honest bind, from both sides: *when we seek to keep all the commandments in our personal lives, we are sometimes accused of having no love for those who don't. When we show personal love and support loving causes, we are sometimes misunderstood as implying support for results that contradict our other religious duties.* The counsel: live peaceably and lovingly with other children of God who do not share our values and have not assumed our covenant obligations; in a democratic government, seek fairness for all. **And the empirical observation: in countless circumstances, strangers' suspicion or even hostility gradually gives way to friendship when personal contacts produce mutual respect.**

**Who is a peacemaker — the concrete list.** A bishop healing a troubled marriage or resolving a controversy · young men and women who forgo self-gratifying activities for service and kindness · those who reduce human suffering and promote understanding among peoples · **faithful mothers and fathers who care for their own children or shelter foster children and raise them in righteousness rather than leave them to be scarred and twisted by the sins of others** · missionaries, who work for peace by preaching repentance from personal corruption, greed, and oppression, **because only by individual reformation can an entire society eventually rise above such evils.**

**Scriptures.** Alma 11:43–44 · Matthew 5:9, 43–44; 22:37–39 · Luke 10:29–37 · 1 John 4:19 · 3 Nephi 12:9 · Mosiah 5:2.
**Hymns/poems.** None.
**Authorities.** David O. McKay — no better way to manifest love for God than unselfish love for one's fellowmen · Howard W. Hunter — the pure love of Christ has no place for bigotry, hatred, or violence, and encourages diverse people to live together in Christian love regardless of religious belief, race, nationality, financial standing, education, or culture · Joseph Smith (Journal, 1843–44, Joseph Smith Papers) — *pour forth love* · Nelson, "Peacemakers Needed" (2023).

**Project alignment — the most important paragraph in this session for the Fortress work.**
- **"Only by individual reformation can an entire society eventually rise above such evils."** This is the Fortress Sequence's core claim, stated by the President of the Church: national outcomes are downstream of individual ones. **It is also the Law of the Root and the micro/macro spectrum of the Nazareth Effect, in one sentence, from the highest available source.** File to *Quotable* and to the Fortress diagnostic's framing.
- **Marker 7 (Cohesion) and Marker 11 (Force / restraint) together.** Loving the adversary is Marker 11's outer limit, and the honest description of the double bind is Marker 7's real cost.
- **Marker 5 (Truth).** *This does not mean surrendering our values* — the peacemaking is not accomplished by conceding the contest.
- **Two Worlds.** The "toxic era" diagnosis, sourced to current writers, and the observation that personal contact dissolves hostility, are directly usable for the secular-audience line.
- **The nurse is the Nazareth Effect at its extreme:** an invitee who is actively repellent, an inviter who despised him, and the change occurring in **the inviter** rather than the invitee. *The transformation ran the wrong direction — and that is the point.*
- **RM value, Wall 11 and Wall 4.** Ben will re-enter a contested world with strong views. This is the address that tells him how to hold them without hardening.

**Takeaways.** (1) Mortal deficiencies are only temporary. (2) The despised man's last words were that he wanted to go home. (3) Society rises only as individuals do.
