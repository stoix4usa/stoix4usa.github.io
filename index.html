Isaiah Tools

Isaiah is hard on purpose. Not because it is badly written and not because it is old — because it is compressed. It is poetry doing three jobs at once, and that was on purpose.

Nephi said his soul delighted in Isaiah, then admitted his own people could not follow it, because “they know not concerning the manner of prophesying among the Jews.” So the difficulty is not a defect. It is a locked gate. Isaiah gives the key to his meaning only to someone who works hard at it, and not to someone who just skims.

Here is the “manner of prophesying among the Jews” in three tools.

The three tools

Tool One

The second line explains the first.

Hebrew poetry rhymes ideas, not sounds. Nearly every line comes in a pair, and the second half is the first half said again with a turn on it.

For my thoughts are not your thoughts, neither are your ways my ways, saith the Lord. Isaiah 55:8

The second half adds no new information. It restates. Once you see that, half of Isaiah stops being fog — you are not missing something, you are reading the same thought twice.

Try it: Isaiah 1:18. Find the two pairs. Scarlet / snow. Crimson / wool.

Tool Two

Ask who else this could be.

Isaiah 7:14 was spoken to King Ahaz about a child born in his own lifetime, as a sign about an invasion. Matthew 1:23 says it is about Christ. Both are true.

Prophecy in Isaiah runs in layers — his day, Christ’s day, the last days. So the right question is never which one is it. It is how many is it.

This is the tool that keeps you from throwing the book across the room.

Tool Three

Watch the pronouns.

Isaiah writes to a whole nation. Plural. Ye. O Israel. My people. Then, with no warning, he drops to singular. Thee. Thou. Him.

Every time he switches, something just got personal. It is never an accident, and it is the tool most people never pick up.

What the third tool opens

Run all three on this verse before you read anything under it.

All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own way; and the Lord hath laid on him the iniquity of us all. Isaiah 53:6

Tool One gives you the pair: gone astray / turned to his own way. Tool Three gives you the thing worth keeping. All we and us all bracket the verse — but the middle of it is every one to his own way.

The wandering is itemized. One person, one direction, one wrong turn at a time. What is laid on Him is not a lump sum. It is your particular way.

Then this one, and read it slowly:

Can a woman forget her sucking child… yea, they may forget, yet will I not forget thee. Behold, I have graven thee upon the palms of my hands. Isaiah 49:15–16

Singular. Not Israel’s name on the palms. And consider what else is on the palms of His hands.

The question it leaves you

If He came for the individual — by name, one at a time — then the next question is not about Him. It is about you.

“It is not even enough to be convinced of the gospel; we must act so that we are converted by it.”

President Dallin H. Oaks, “Kingdoms of Glory,” October 2023

Notice what just happened. You were convinced by Isaiah 49:16 — you saw it, it holds, the argument is good. Oaks says that is not the finish line. Convinced is something that happens to you. Converted is something you do.

Two fair questions

Why would God hide something this important behind difficulty?

He did not hide it. He priced it. Anything you can get without effort you can also lose without noticing.

Did Isaiah really mean Christ, or are we reading that back into him?

Isaiah 7:14 genuinely was about Ahaz’s day. Matthew genuinely applies it to Christ. Nephi says that is how the manner of prophesying works. Layers are not a cheat — they are the form.
