Honor and Power

Every war starts with competing parties claiming the same thing as their own. The War in Heaven starts with a demand (claim) so short that it is easy to miss when reading: Lucifer (Satan) asks God for a thing that turns out not to be a thing at all — it is to become Him. Here is that story in three movements.

Three movements

Movement One

The claim, in his own words.

Christ himself names the moment it happened in D&C 29:36:

He rebelled against me, saying, “Give me thine honor”, which is my power; and also a third part of the hosts of heaven turned he away from me because of their agency. D&C 29:36

Isaiah wrote this story centuries before D&C 29 was revealed and it is the same voice, making the same demand:

How art thou fallen from heaven, O Lucifer, son of the morning!… For thou hast said in thine heart, I will ascend into heaven, I will exalt my throne above the stars of God… I will be like the most High. Isaiah 14:12–14

Read D&C 29:36 again. Lucifer was not asking for a compliment. He thought he was clever enough to trick God himself. He knew that God’s power and status as the Supreme Being came from His unblemished integrity. Rather than discipline himself to do the hard work required of perfect integrity, he demanded that God grant him His power as a reward for volunteering to save mankind. And his plan was based on breaking the Father’s most cherished law: let each one choose me because they love me and want to honor me. Satan asks for honor, that is freely given by those who feel loved, and plans to use it to gain power by force, which would break the trust needed for honor to exist. He knew that honor and power are the same thing and was attempting to corrupt honor for his own power grab.

Movement Two

Why the claim was never possible.

Twice in Isaiah, God states the rule Lucifer was breaking — not as a threat, but as a fact about how honor works:

I am the Lord: that is my name: and my glory will I not give to another, neither my praise to graven images. Isaiah 42:8
For mine own sake… for how should my name be polluted? and I will not give my glory unto another. Isaiah 48:11

Notice this is not God refusing to share. His glory is the honor His children give Him freely, each one by their own agency, because His integrity is perfect and they trust Him. That kind of honor cannot be handed to someone else. It is not God’s to hand over — it is theirs to give. You cannot give away the trust your friends place in you, either. That is exactly why Lucifer’s demand could never succeed. He wasn’t refused a gift. He demanded something only other people’s agency can give, and he planned to take their agency away to get it. That is why he was cast down. Look again at how D&C 29:36 ends: a third part of heaven turned away because of their agency. Honor follows agency, in both directions.

Movement Three

The same test, now ours.

Paul says that this power grab did not end in heaven. He calls it the mystery of iniquity — still working, in people deceived the same way Lucifer was:

For the mystery of iniquity doth already work… even him, . . . Satan with all power and signs and lying wonders, and with all deceivableness of unrighteousness in them that perish; because they received not the love of the truth, that they might be saved. 2 Thessalonians 2:7–10

Read what actually damns them: not that the truth was hidden, but that they received it not. They withheld trust the way Lucifer tried to seize honor — a small motion in the same direction. Christ says the same thing about who can and cannot be saved:

…even as many as have loved me and kept my commandments, and none else. D&C 29:12
…my blood shall not cleanse them if they hear me not. D&C 29:17
…where I am they cannot come, for they have no power. D&C 29:29

There is the same path, run in the other direction. Lucifer tried to take honor that was never his, and ended up with no power. Verse 29 says a person who will not trust and love Christ ends up in the identical place — no power — not as a punishment handed down, but for the same reason Lucifer’s grab failed. Power was never a separate prize you could win by force or lose by refusal. It was always the natural result of trust placed in the One who merits it by His perfect integrity. When you withhold trust, there is nothing left to carry the power.

What “damned” actually means here

To be damned is not a judgment imposed. It is a description of what withheld trust produces as a natural result.

A dam does not punish a river. It stops it — that is the whole word. Someone who will not love and trust Christ enough to follow Him has not been denied power as a penalty from outside. They have dammed their own channel from the inside, the same smaller motion that Lucifer made when he asked for what could not be transferred away from its source.

Which is why the question this page leaves you with is not do you believe the doctrine? It is: where, this week, are you withholding the trust that the power was waiting on to come into your life?

Two fair questions

Isn’t it unfair that not trusting Christ costs you power you never had a chance to use?

The power was never independent of Him to begin with — it is His life, in you, the way light in a room is the lamp’s, not the room’s. Withholding trust doesn’t confiscate a power you already own; it declines the connection that delivers the power. Nothing is taken that was yours separate from Him.

Is this saying Isaiah 14 is only about Lucifer, and not about the king of Babylon it names?

No — it is both, the same layering Isaiah 7:14 uses for Ahaz and Christ at once. The taunt is aimed at a real, arrogant king. Scripture uses that king’s fall as the visible shadow of a larger one behind it — which is exactly how D&C 29:36 can quote Isaiah’s words as Lucifer’s own.
